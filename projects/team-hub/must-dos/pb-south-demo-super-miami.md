# Must Dos · PB South demo-to-vertical superintendent

**The seat:** a GC superintendent, not someone from a demolition contractor, who has been around demo on a few past projects (taking a building to the ground, then going back up). They run the demo, then stay on the team that takes a 40-50 floor tower vertical, right across the street from Project X. Smart and scalable beats deep demo experience.

## The scorecard (8 Must Dos)

| # | Must Do | What "meets" looks like |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | GC superintendent who has had demolition on past projects | Demo, then back up vertical on the same job; GC side, not a demo contractor |
| 2 | Leads subcontractors through demolition scopes | Has directed demo subs; if not directly, shows on the call they grasp scopes 3 to 6 fast |
| 3 | Hazard analysis on demolition scopes | Has run (daily) hazard analyses on demo work and can walk through one |
| 4 | Knows demo safety protocols | Can name what to look for: shoring, utilities, falling debris, exclusion zones, engineering surveys |
| 5 | Good neighbour in a dense urban site | Knows why dust control, vibration, noise and protecting adjacent buildings matter in this phase |
| 6 | Has managed demo subcontractors | Names which demo subs, and how they kept them in line |
| 7 | Gone vertical on large high-rises in SE Florida | Names the towers, floors and their role |
| 8 | Stays for the vertical, and can grow | Wants the full run from demo to top-out; sharp, coachable, mid-career (about 10 to 20 years in the field) |

## Screening questions (ask every candidate)

1. Which high-rise projects have you been on?
2. What was your role on each: seniority, and what did you own?
3. Any projects start to finish? Otherwise, how long were you on each site?
4. Which demo subcontractors have you managed?
5. Walk me through a hazard analysis you ran on a demo scope.
6. How did you handle dust and the neighbours on an urban demo?
