# Must Dos · PB South Vertical Transportation Specialist (Miami)

**The seat:** the owner-side elevator expert on a very large high-rise elevator package. They protect PB South's schedule and money on the most schedule-critical system in the tower: design review, submittals, the install sequence, temporary or construction elevators, testing and the turnover to the AHJ. They are not a general CM, and they are not an elevator mechanic. Two profiles fit:
- **VT Engineer / consultant** (WSP, Lerch Bates, Syska, VDA, JH): strong on design, traffic and code.
- **VT Project Manager from an OEM** (Otis, KONE, Schindler, TK Elevator): strong on install, schedule and the field.

## The scorecard (8 Must Dos)

| # | Must Do | What "meets" looks like |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Elevator expertise on high-rise new construction | 30+ floor towers (residential, office or hotel). Names the project, the number of cars and the speeds. New install, not only modernization. |
| 2 | Owner-side or OEM construction-lead role | VT consultant, OEM construction manager, PM or installation superintendent. Not a GC generalist, and not only service or sales. |
| 3 | Reads and challenges the design | Has reviewed shop drawings and submittals. Knows hoistway, pit and overhead, machine room vs MRL, and the structural and electrical tie-ins. |
| 4 | Code fluency | ASME A17.1 / CSA B44, the A17.2 inspection guide, and the Florida Building Code elevator provisions. Has dealt with Miami-Dade and Florida inspectors (the AHJ). QEI certification is a plus. |
| 5 | Schedule and sequence on a live tower | Has planned hoistway turnover, rail and car installation and construction or temporary elevators (or hoists) around the GC schedule, and protected the top-out to TCO path. |
| 6 | Testing, acceptance and closeout | Has witnessed acceptance and safety tests, run punch lists, and closed out with the AHJ and the building owner. |
| 7 | Commercial sense | Has handled change orders, bids or bid leveling, and backcharges with the elevator subcontractor. Can explain how they protected the owner's money. |
| 8 | South Florida or ready to be there | Lives in SE Florida or will relocate (relocation is open per John). Comfortable full time on site in Miami. |

Nice to have: destination dispatch, traffic analysis or simulation, double-deck or high-speed (over 1,000 fpm) cars, and supertall experience.

## Screening questions (ask every candidate)

1. Which high-rise elevator projects have you run? For each: floors, number of cars, speeds, and your role.
2. Owner side, consultant side or OEM side? What did you personally own: design, submittals, install, testing?
3. Walk me through how you sequence elevator installation on a 40+ floor tower. When do hoistways turn over, and how do you handle construction elevators?
4. Which codes do you work to? Any QEI or other certification? Which inspectors (AHJ) have you closed out with, and any in Florida?
5. Tell me about a time the elevator package threatened the schedule. What did you do?
6. How have you handled change orders or backcharges with the elevator subcontractor?
7. Are you in South Florida? If not, is relocating to Miami workable, and when could you start?
8. What do you earn now (base, bonus, vehicle), and what would make a move worth it? (Internal only. Never on a client document.)

**Stop signs:** only service or maintenance work, only sales, no new-install high-rise experience, or "elevators were one of my trades as a GC PM" with no technical depth.

## The pitch (for recruiters)

- **Scale:** a very large elevator scope on a Miami high-rise with PB South, a builder running some of the biggest towers in the market. This is the seat where elevators are their whole job, not a side job.
- **Why move:** an owner-side voice on a landmark tower, with the authority to get the elevator plan right from the start instead of chasing it.
- Never call Related staff (PB South's partner on Project X). That is a standing rule.
