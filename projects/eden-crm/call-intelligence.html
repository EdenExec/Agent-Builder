Eden Call Intelligence

What Jordan's team does with call data, and how Eden puts it to work. Every step after the first depends on CloudTalk recording our calls.

Radar, 11 October 2026. Source: "The Rag Podcast", interview with Jordan, founder of Atlas, an AI-first recruiting CRM (not our tutor Atlas). 88 minutes, every line of the transcript read. Likely recorded around 2024 and sponsored by Atlas, so treat feature claims as dated and partly promotional.

The point

  • Every call becomes data. Recruiters stop typing notes; the AI listens, fills the record and writes the note minutes after the call ends.
  • That data then does the work: new leads from managers mentioned on calls, search across everything anyone has said, ratios from real activity.
  • None of it happens until our calls are recorded and reachable. CloudTalk is step one, as you said.

Three decisions for Kev

Record every Eden call in CloudTalk

My pickYes, with a short recorded notice at the start of each call. Florida and Washington generally require every party's consent, so counsel confirms the notice wording before it goes on.

Keep work calls inside CloudTalk

My pickYes. Recruiters dial and answer in CloudTalk, and you use the CloudTalk app on your phone. A call forwarded out to Google Voice may lose its recording and transcript (Matias confirms). Radar then reads missed calls and voicemails straight from CloudTalk, which replaces the 11 October plan to route everything to Google Voice for work calls.

One call pipe, shared by Q and Rhaven

My pickYes, if Cody agrees. Q pulls CloudTalk calls nightly into Eden's own database, next to the Crelate copy. Rhaven reads from that pipe instead of building a second one, so every tool sees the same calls.

The plan

Step 1
This week

CloudTalk goes live

  • Send Matias the six questions below.
  • Turn on recording and transcription for every line once the notice is approved.
  • Create a CloudTalk API key and add it to this environment's secrets (never paste it in chat). Q takes it from there.
  • Each recruiter writes down a one-week estimate of admin hours, so we can measure the change.

Gate: your yes on decisions 1 and 2, and the plan and price from Matias.

Step 2
Weeks 1–2

Calls land on the record

  • Q pulls every call nightly: who, when, how long, which recruiter, the outcome.
  • Calls match to Crelate contacts by phone number. Unknown numbers go to Radar's call-back list.
  • The scoreboard gets real numbers: calls and talk time per recruiter, call to submittal to interview to placement.
Step 3
Weeks 2–4

Notes write themselves

  • Three templates, drafted by Radar for your edit: candidate screen, client business-development call, interview debrief.
  • Q tests them on ten real transcripts and a recruiter checks each one.
  • Then every call gets its note, saved to Crelate as a draft the recruiter approves (no automatic writes into Crelate at first).
Step 4
Month 2

Calls feed the hunt

  • Managers named on calls go to Scout's lead queue, checked against Crelate and the client list.
  • Search across everything said on our calls, with the reason for each match.
  • Radar drafts three-bullet candidate pitches to hiring managers for your send.
  • Measure the admin hours again against the Step 1 estimate.

Six questions for Matias at CloudTalk

  1. Can every inbound and outbound call on all Eden lines be recorded automatically, with a consent message played at the start?
  2. Which plan includes AI transcription and call summaries (Conversation Intelligence), and what does it cost for our seats?
  3. Can our system pull call records, recordings and transcripts through the API, and can CloudTalk notify us when each call ends instead of us checking?
  4. If a call is forwarded to a cell phone or Google Voice, is it still recorded and transcribed?
  5. Can your AI voice agent answer calls we miss, take a message and pass it to us?
  6. Does CloudTalk log calls into Crelate, directly or through Zapier?
