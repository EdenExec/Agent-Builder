## 1. Bottom line

What Jordan is doing that Eden should copy, most important first.

1. **Treat every call as data and stop asking recruiters to take notes.** Atlas (Jordan's CRM) is built on the idea that recruiting runs on "phone calls, emails, and PDFs" [03:42], and that if everything the agency "said, heard, read, or wrote" is captured, any question about a candidate or client gets "the full intelligence of your company immediately" [04:29]. The podcast's ad read says notes and a transcript arrive "minutes after a call ends" [38:36]. For Eden this only works once calls are recorded at the source, which is why CloudTalk has to be live first.
2. **Let AI fill the fields from the conversation, using templates the recruiter writes.** When someone says a salary, Atlas (Jordan's CRM) puts it in the salary field "as soon as you put the phone down" [64:01]. Jordan writes his own template for business-development calls ("Were there any managers mentioned?") and applies it to every call [64:41]. The AI goes back over the call and writes notes to the template's instructions [65:12]. Data entry becomes a by-product of the call.
3. **Have AI tag and search, not people.** Resumes are tagged by industry and function on arrival, whatever words the candidate used [59:51]. Recruiters can define custom AI tags in plain words (a stated roadmap item) [60:49] and search for anyone who "has ever uttered the term", with an AI explanation of why each person matches [61:44]. This maps straight onto Eden's tagging system and resume search (brief #3, #8, #11).
4. **Use the full record to write short, specific outreach.** Jordan's spec workflow (on the roadmap) takes a job description plus "every call we've had, every interview we've done, every note, every email, every resume" for a candidate and writes three bullet points on why that person fits [54:50]. He says doing this by hand in ChatGPT takes about twenty minutes [55:26]. This is better outreach to hiring managers, built from Eden's own call history.
5. **Count the admin minutes, then remove them one at a time. Embed rather than build where you can.** Jordan's case is that if half a recruiter's time is admin, removing it could "theoretically make twice the revenue" [58:54]. He ships small time-savers: one key dials the profile on screen [45:56], one key turns a note into a call log [50:32], and scheduling pre-fills the slots agreed on the call ("three minutes saved there, three minutes saved here") [66:32]. He embeds a dashboard product (MetaBase) instead of building his own, because "dashboarding is really complex" [72:28].
