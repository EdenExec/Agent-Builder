Subject: The Name Game: how to play it

To: Recruiting team (Max, Evan, Noah, Andrew, Bridger); cc Nick

Team,

Thanks for the energy this morning. Here is how to play the Name Game, so you can run it on your own.

The point is simple. People trust specifics. "I just spoke with [name], who runs precon at [firm], about [project]" earns the next question. "I work with a lot of GCs" doesn't. The game trains you to remember names, place them in a project, and use them on calls the way a consultant would.

How to play (5 minutes, pen only, no laptop):

1. Grab the Name Game page from this morning's handout. Two boxes: My Market and My Client.
2. Set a 5-minute timer. Fill in My Market from memory: your top 5 firms, the precon or ops leader at each, and one live project each.
3. Under it, write who just moved and where.
4. Fill in My Client: the people who decide, what each cares about this quarter, and their three biggest competitors for talent.
5. Now open Crelate and your depth chart. In a second colour, fill every gap. The gaps are your homework.
6. Pick one name and use it on a call today, with a line on why it matters to the person you are calling.

A quick example. You write down "Pat, precon manager at [firm], working a hospital GMP" and you remember it. Later you open a call with a candidate: "Before we get into the role, I was just talking with Pat, who runs precon at [firm], about a hospital GMP. Here is what we are seeing in your market this month." Then stop and listen.

Ground rules: names and roles only. No comp, no private details, and never a client's name in front of another client. A name you can't place in a project doesn't count.

How often: every Friday as our warm-up, and any time you want a quick rep at the end of a Power Hour. Your blanks should shrink each week. If they don't, that tells you where to spend tomorrow's calls.

Kev
