Why today. Clients buy RPO when they can see every job order being worked, and they keep paying when we show up with intel, not just résumés. Crelate is the proof; market knowledge is the edge.

1 · Crelate best practices 8:02 · 15 min

Laptops open. Use the Hub's Job Orders folder to see who's on what.

  • Activity to the right job: this week's RPs, PTCs, screens and submittals are logged against the JO they were for. Re-link anything filed to the contact only.
  • One board standard: same stage names in order, every candidate in exactly one stage, owner set, a next step with a date, and a why-submitted note on every submittal.
  • Name one fix you'll finish by end of day. Nick audits the boards Friday.
The RPO line

"Here's every JO, the activity on it this week, and where each candidate stands." If Crelate can't show it, it didn't happen.

Ask on the call

"Do you build budgets from SD drawings or price hard-bid CDs?" · "Who's in the owner meetings?" · "GMP or lump sum?" A "precon manager" who only does takeoffs is an estimator.

3 · Become the consultant 8:29 · 12 min

Every client and candidate call carries one piece of intel and one piece of advice.

  • Bring a number: backlog, labor, what titles are paying, who's hiring for data centers. One line, sourced.
  • Bring the map: who's available, who just moved, who competitors are chasing. Your depth chart is your briefing.
  • Give a recommendation: "If you open this to remote precon you'll double the pool," or "Your title reads estimator; call it Precon Manager and you'll reach the right people."
Opener

"Before we get into the role, here's what we're seeing in your market this month…" Then stop and listen.

Rep (5 min, pen)

Write the 3-sentence intel brief you'll give your top client this week: one number, one observation, one recommendation.
