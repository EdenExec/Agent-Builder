# Part 4. Intern Compliance Training

**The one rule:** at Eden, every intern is a paid, hourly W-2 employee.

### Summary

Pay every hour, track every hour and promise nothing in writing that is not in a signed plan. Follow those three habits and almost every intern risk goes away.

### 1. Intern, employee or contractor (5 minutes)

| | Unpaid intern | Employee (our interns) | Independent contractor |
|---|---|---|---|
| Who it really is | A student whose training benefits **them** more than us | Anyone we direct, train and schedule to do our work | A separate business that controls **how** it does the job |
| Test | Primary beneficiary (DOL Fact Sheet #71, January 2018) | The default when someone works for pay | DOL economic reality; IRS control test; Idaho: free from control **and** has its own established business (Idaho Code 72-1316(4)) |
| Pay | None | At least $7.25 an hour for every hour, plus overtime over 40 hours a week | Invoices for results |
| At Eden | **Not used.** Our interns do real, fee-producing work. | **All four pathways** | **Never for interns.** Training, schedules and our tools make them employees. |

The label decides nothing. "Intern", "stipend" and "1099" on paper do not change what the job actually is.

### 2. The seven primary-beneficiary factors, with Eden examples (7 minutes)

This test only matters if someone wants an intern unpaid. We don't. Learn it so we never slide into unpaid work ("come shadow for a week", "help out before your start date").

| # | Factor (DOL Fact Sheet #71) | Eden example | Points to |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Both sides understand there is no pay | We pay by the hour | Employee |
| 2 | Training like a classroom or clinic | Weekly headhunter training is real training, but the rest of the week is live dialing | Mixed |
| 3 | Tied to school through coursework or credit | Some interns may get Boise State credit | Intern, but only partly |
| 4 | Fits the academic calendar | Semester hours built around classes | Intern, but only partly |
| 5 | Lasts only as long as the learning does | Semester terms with end dates | Intern, if we keep end dates |
| 6 | Complements paid staff rather than displacing them | Client Success does "tasks that would normally be commissionable" | **Employee** |
| 7 | No promise of a job at the end | "A path to full time" in the draft | **Employee**, until we reword it |

**Takeaway.** Factor 6 alone means these jobs must be paid. That is fine, because we pay.

### 3. Words to use and words to avoid (6 minutes)

Applies to postings, offers, Slack, texts, interviews and casual conversation. Slack is evidence.

| Avoid | Use instead | Why |
|---|---|---|
| "Stipend" | "Hourly pay" | A stipend sounds like not-wages |
| "1099", "contractor", "freelance" (about interns) | "Part-time employee", "intern" | They are employees |
| "Unpaid", "volunteer", "trial shift", "shadow day" | "Paid interview day" (if any time is spent working) | Any work time is paid time |
| "You'll be full time after this", "path to full time" | "Strong interns may be considered for open roles" | An implied promise of a job |
| "Performance incentives", "you'll earn on every deal" | "Commission under the written plan", or nothing | A promised bonus is wages, and Idaho can award 3x the unpaid amount |
| "College students", "recent grads", "young", "digital native" | "Students welcome", "early in your career or changing careers" | Age discrimination (29 CFR 1625.4(a)) |
| "Ideally a woman (or man)", "he or she would fit the culture" | Describe the work, not the person | Sex discrimination (42 USC 2000e-3(b)) |
| Asking about faith, church, family plans, age or graduation year | Ask about the work: "Tell me about a time you..." | Religion, sex, age |
| "Just finish it tonight, don't clock it" | "Log every hour, and get approval first if you'll pass 40" | Off-the-clock work is a wage claim |
| "You own the 90-day reviews" (to the Operations Intern) | "You schedule and track them; leaders write them" | Confidentiality and authority |

### 4. Hours and timekeeping (2 minutes)

- Every minute of work is paid: training, team meetings, calls from home, Slack after hours that a leader asked for. Training counts as work unless it is outside hours, truly voluntary, unrelated to the job **and** involves no productive work (29 CFR 785.27). Ours never meets all four.
- Interns record time in Gusto, a supervisor approves it weekly, and no one edits an intern's time without telling them in writing. Summer full-time interns need written approval before going past 40 hours.
- Overtime is 1.5x the regular rate over 40 hours in a workweek, and the regular rate includes commission. Example: 45 hours at $15 plus $450 commission gives a regular rate of $25.00, so the overtime premium is $62.50 and the week's pay is $1,187.50.

### 5. School credit (1 minute)

- Credit is between the student and the school. Eden fills in the school's employer forms and pays the same rate either way.
- Before anyone signs a school agreement, Docket reads it. School agreements often carry indemnity and insurance clauses.
- "Capstone" projects that use Eden data need a confidentiality agreement and a leader's sign-off on what leaves the building.

### 6. Ending an internship (2 minutes)

| Step | Rule |
|---|---|
| End date | The internship ends on its stated date; extending it takes a signed writing |
| Final pay | Due by the next regular payday or within 10 days (weekends and holidays excluded), whichever is first; 48 hours if the intern asks in writing (Idaho Code 45-606) |
| Commission | Earned commission paid under the plan; unearned commission follows the plan, never a verbal deal |
| Exit | Collect laptop, badge and system access on the last day; remind the intern in writing of the confidentiality agreement |
| Unemployment | Expect claims and answer them truthfully; W-2 interns make this routine |

### 8. Quiz (5 questions)

**1. A Boise State student offers to "help out for free for two weeks" to see if she likes recruiting. Can we accept?**
No. She would be doing our work, and we are for-profit. Pay her hourly from her first hour, or don't have her work.

**2. A Sales Intern works 45 hours in a summer week at $15 an hour and is paid $450 of commission that week. What does Eden owe?**
$1,187.50. The regular rate is ($675 + $450) / 45 = $25.00, and the 5 overtime hours earn a $12.50 premium each ($62.50).

**3. Can we pay Sales Interns as 1099 contractors if we call the money a stipend?**
No. We train, schedule, supervise and equip them on our searches, so they are employees under DOL, IRS and Idaho tests alike. The word "stipend" changes nothing.

**4. Nick says in Slack, "Whoever crushes it this semester gets the open recruiter seat." What is the problem, and what should he say?**
It is a written promise of a job and of a performance-based reward. Say instead: "Strong interns may be considered for open roles."

**5. The Operations Intern asks to read a candidate's background report to "double check it." Yes or no?**
No. The intern tracks status only. Report contents go to a leader, who follows the FCRA steps (standalone disclosure and authorisation, then a pre-adverse action notice with a copy of the report, 15 USC 1681b(b)).
