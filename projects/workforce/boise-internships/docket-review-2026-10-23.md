# Verdict: FIX FIRST, then NEEDS COUNSEL on three points

## Executive summary

The postings are close. Paying every intern by the hour as a W-2 employee is the right model. It takes the FLSA intern test out of play for pay. Ten lines need rewording before anything goes on Handshake. Three items need an Idaho employment lawyer before the first offer goes out: the Sales Intern commission plan, the background check workflow, and remote recruiters in other states.

## Top three issues (ranked by dollars at risk times likelihood)

| # | Issue | Where | What it costs if it goes wrong |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Sales Interns as 1099 contractors (Kev's idea) | Nick's notes; Pathway B pay line | Not defensible under any of the four tests. Back payroll taxes, Idaho unemployment and workers' comp exposure. The bigger risk: an intern's unemployment claim at semester end starts an Idaho audit that also looks at the existing 1099 recruiting team. See `docket-1099-and-offer-letters-2026-10-23.md`. |
| 2 | Commission and "performance incentives" promised with no written plan | Pathways B and C; Posting 2 | Idaho counts commissions as wages (Idaho Code 45-601). An unpaid commission dispute can cost three times the unpaid amount plus the intern's attorney fees (45-615). Commissions also raise the overtime rate (29 CFR 778.117). |
| 3 | Operations Intern handling background checks, drug tests and employee evaluations | Pathway A duties | FCRA disclosure and adverse-action failures are a common class-action target. If Eden orders reports and passes them to clients, Eden may itself count as a consumer reporting agency (15 USC 1681a(f)). Leaked drug-test or evaluation data is a privacy and morale loss that is hard to reverse. |

## 1. The legal frame in one page

**Paid W-2 interns are simply part-time employees.** The "intern" label changes nothing about pay. The FLSA primary-beneficiary test only decides whether someone can be left **unpaid**. DOL Fact Sheet #71 (updated January 2018, read 23 October 2026) says an intern who is an employee "is entitled to both minimum wage and overtime pay." The Ninth Circuit, which covers Idaho, uses the same seven-factor test (*Benjamin v. B&H Education*, 877 F.3d 1139 (9th Cir. 2017), adopting *Glatt*). **SETTLED.**

**How each pathway fares under the test.** These are real, revenue-producing jobs, so the test would find all four pathways to be employment. That is fine as long as Eden pays all of them.

| Pathway | Who benefits most | Unpaid would be | Paid hourly W-2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Operations | Eden: it takes work off three leaders' plates, and Nick's notes call it "busy work and one-offs" | Unlawful | Compliant |
| Sales (Jr. Headhunter) | Eden: sourcing and dialing on live, fee-bearing searches | Unlawful | Compliant, with a written commission plan |
| Client Success | Eden: Nick's notes call these "tasks that would normally be commissionable" (factor 6, it displaces paid work) | Unlawful | Compliant |
| Marketing | Mixed, but the decks and posts are used to win clients | High risk | Compliant |

**Minimum wage and overtime.** Federal and Idaho minimum wage is $7.25 an hour. Idaho Code 44-1502(1) tracks the federal rate. **SETTLED.** Idaho has no overtime law of its own, so the FLSA applies: time and a half over 40 hours in a workweek, at a "regular rate" that includes commissions and promised bonuses (29 CFR 778.117; 778.211(b)). **SETTLED.** Paid training and the weekly headhunter session count as work time, because they fail every one of the four exclusion criteria in 29 CFR 785.27. **SETTLED.** The summer is the danger zone: full-time hours plus a commission week produces overtime that has to be computed correctly.

**Idaho also allows a $4.25 youth wage** for workers under 20 in their first 90 days (44-1502(3)). It is lawful but not recommended: the pay would differ between interns in the same cohort, and it would hurt the program's reputation. **SETTLED** (text).

**Inside sales is not exempt.** Phone sourcing from the office does not fit the outside sales exemption: "Outside sales does not include sales made by mail, telephone or the Internet" (29 CFR 541.502). **SETTLED.** Interns are non-exempt, so every hour must be tracked.

### Commission-only or stipend-only: workable?

| Model | Workable? | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Commission only | **No.** Do not use it for interns. | Placements close 60 to 120 days out, so most weeks show $0 commission. Eden would still owe $7.25 or more for every hour, every workweek. Any draw recovered later cannot push a week below minimum wage, because wages must be paid "free and clear" (29 CFR 531.35). Recovering a draw also needs the employee's written authorisation (Idaho Code 45-609). |
| Flat stipend only (for example, $X a month) | Lawful only if Eden still tracks hours and tops up any week where the stipend divided by hours falls below $7.25, plus overtime. | It saves no admin and creates a trap in heavy weeks. Call it an hourly wage instead. |
| Hourly wage plus a written commission plan | **Recommended.** | The hourly wage is the minimum-wage floor and the commission sits on top. Overtime is computed on the blended rate. |

### Idaho wage payment rules that shape the commission plan

| Rule | Idaho Code | Grade |
|---|---|---|
| Commissions are wages ("time, task, piece or commission basis") | 45-601 | SETTLED |
| Payday at least monthly, no more than 15 days after the pay period ends | 45-608 | SETTLED |
| Final pay by the earlier of the next payday or 10 days (weekends and holidays excluded); 48 hours on written request | 45-606(1) | SETTLED |
| Late-pay penalty: up to 15 days' pay, capped at $750 ($500 if paid before a lien), or 3x the unpaid wages plus fees in court | 45-607, 45-615 | SETTLED |
| Claims within 2 years (12 months for extra pay on a paid period) | 45-614 | SETTLED |
| Written commission plan required by statute | none found | UNSURE, drives nothing |

Write the plan anyway. Without one, the 3x remedy applies to whatever an intern says was promised. With one, the plan's definition of "earned" controls.

## 3. Operations Intern: background checks, drug tests and evaluations

- **FCRA (15 USC 1681b(b)).** Before Eden gets a background report on a job applicant, it must give a disclosure "in a document that consists solely of the disclosure" and get written authorisation. Before taking adverse action, it must send a copy of the report and the summary of rights. **SETTLED.** An intern can send the vendor's invitations. An intern should not decide anything, read reports or send adverse-action letters.
- **Whose checks are these?** If Eden orders checks for **its own** hires, Eden is a "user", and the rules above cover it. If Eden orders checks on candidates **for clients** and passes the reports along, Eden may be "assembling or evaluating... for the purpose of furnishing consumer reports to third parties", which is the definition of a consumer reporting agency (15 USC 1681a(f)). **SETTLED** (text); whether it applies to Eden is **UNSURE**. **OUTSIDE COUNSEL.**
- **Drug tests and evaluations.** A test for illegal drugs is not a medical exam under the ADA (42 USC 12114(d)). **SETTLED.** Lab reports can still reveal prescriptions, so keep results in a separate confidential file with leader-only access. Idaho's voluntary drug-free workplace statute (Idaho Code 72-1701 et seq.) was not read this session (**UNSURE**), so it drives nothing here. Leaders write, sign and hold evaluations and scorecards. The intern schedules them and tracks completion. Every intern signs Eden's confidentiality agreement before getting any system access.

## 4. Referral bonus (to employees and non-employees)

| Paid to | Treatment | Source |
|---|---|---|
| An Eden employee | Taxable wages through Gusto. For non-exempt staff it may stay out of the overtime rate only for "employees not primarily engaged in recruiting activities" (29 CFR 778.211(d)). Sales and Client Success interns **are** engaged in recruiting, so for them it counts in the overtime rate. | SETTLED |
| Anyone else | Not wages. Collect a W-9. Form 1099-NEC is required once payments to that person reach $2,000 in a year (the 2026 threshold under the 2025 OBBBA; LIKELY, secondary sources, so confirm with Joel and Chris). | LIKELY |
| An employee of an Eden client | Lawful for Eden, but it can breach the client's own rules. Exclude it, or require the referrer to confirm their employer allows it. | Drafting judgment |

**One-page referral terms.** Define what counts as a referral (in writing, first in time), eligible roles (Eden's own posted roles only, not client placements), 90 days of continuous employment by the **hire**, payment date (next regular payday or within 30 days after day 90), exclusions (the hiring leader, agencies, Eden's own recruiters for roles they are filling), and that Eden may change the program for future referrals. Do not make payment depend on the **referrer** staying employed. DOL treats that as a longevity bonus (FLSA2020-4; LIKELY, secondary).

## 5. EEO and the dropped "ideally a woman" note

- Leaving it out of the posting was right. Title VII bars an employer **or employment agency** from publishing an ad "indicating any preference... based on... sex" unless sex is a bona fide occupational qualification (42 USC 2000e-3(b)). **SETTLED.** Admin work has no such qualification. Idaho's Human Rights Act reaches employers with 5 or more employees and employment agencies (Idaho Code 67-5902). **SETTLED** (definitions).
- The note still exists in Nick's notes and in the doc that copies them. Do not delete records now. The doc already records that the note was rejected, which helps Eden. Keep it out of the hiring packet, interview guides and Slack, and never say it in an interview. The training covers this.
- A preference spoken in an interview is as damaging as one in writing, and an interviewer's note of it is evidence.

## 7. What goes to outside employment counsel (Idaho, with multi-state reach)

| Topic | The three questions to ask |
|---|---|
| A. Sales Intern and Recruiter commission plans | (1) Is our "earned" definition (fee collected and guarantee period passed) enforceable under Idaho Code 45-601 to 45-617, including forfeiting unearned commissions at separation? (2) Is our overtime regular-rate method for commissions correct? (3) What must change for remote recruiters in Washington, Oregon or other states? |
| B. Background checks and drug tests | (1) In client searches, could Eden be a consumer reporting agency or reseller under 15 USC 1681a? | C. Existing 1099 recruiting team (spotlight risk) | (1) Under Idaho Code 72-1316(4), are any current contractors employees? (2) Is the IRS Voluntary Classification Settlement Program the right fix? (3) How do we convert them without admitting past liability? |
