# DRAFT FOR ATTORNEY REVIEW

**Verdict: FIX FIRST + NEEDS COUNSEL.** Dean's call is right. Use a one-way confidentiality and non-use agreement with a narrow no-circumvention clause. Do not use a noncompete. Have counsel read it once before 9 November. Confidence: LIKELY.

## 1. Verdict for Kev

**What you wanted:** enough protection to open the books. **What gives it:** promises not to disclose, not to use, and not to go around Eden. A noncompete adds no protection here and creates three problems.

- **A noncompete does not fit a candidate.** Idaho allows noncompetes only for a "key employee or key independent contractor" and only "after termination of employment" (Idaho Code 44-2701, 44-2702, read 2 Nov 2026). A candidate is neither. Wyoming (our home state) and, from 30 June 2027, Washington void most noncompetes (W.S. 1-23-108; ESHB 1155, register R-010). Grade: SETTLED (Idaho text); LIKELY (that a court would refuse one here).
- **This agreement protects what matters.** It covers client names, live job orders, candidate pipelines, pricing, playbooks, Eden University and AI tooling. For 12 months it bars the candidate from using what they saw to work a disclosed client or candidate outside Eden. It leaves them free to take any job, so it reads as fair and is more likely to hold. Grade: LIKELY.
- **Show less, not just sign more.** The paper is the weaker defence. Show the work live on screen, with no copies. Redact candidate names, pay and contact details from depth charts and pipelines. Those candidates trusted Eden with their data, and an NDA does not make it fine to show it to an outsider.

## 2. Counsel questions (OUTSIDE COUNSEL)

1. Is the 12-month no-circumvention clause (section 5) enforceable in Idaho against a non-employee? Does Washington law reach a Zoom candidate who lives in Washington?
2. Eden Partner Group LLC is not registered in Idaho (Docket check, 27 Oct 2026). Must it register as a foreign LLC (Idaho Code 30-21-502) before it can sue on this in Ada County? Grade: UNSURE.
3. Are the NLRA, SEC, Speak Out Act and DTSA carve-outs in section 6 adequate for an applicant? Applicants count as "employees" under the NLRA (*Phelps Dodge v. NLRB*, 313 U.S. 177 (1941)). Grade: LIKELY, not re-read this session.

# Candidate Confidentiality Agreement

**EDEN PARTNER GROUP**

This agreement is between **Eden Partner Group LLC**, a Wyoming limited liability company (**"Eden"**), and **[Candidate name]** (**"you"**), dated **[Date]**.

**1. Why this agreement exists.** You are a candidate for a role with Eden. To help you decide, and to help Eden decide, Eden will show you non-public parts of its business during interviews and related conversations (the **"Process"**). You are not an employee or contractor of Eden, and this agreement does not promise you a job.

**2. Confidential Information.** "Confidential Information" means non-public information that Eden or its people disclose to you in the Process, in any form, including information shown on screen or spoken. It includes Eden's clients and prospects, job orders, fees and pricing, candidates and pipelines, depth charts, playbooks and scripts, training and Eden University material, software and AI tools, prompts and workflows, financial information, and plans. It does not include information that (a) is or becomes public through no fault of yours, (b) you knew before the Process without a duty of confidence, (c) you receive from someone else who is free to share it, or (d) you develop on your own without using Confidential Information.

**3. Your promises.** You will:

(a) keep Confidential Information confidential and not disclose it to anyone, except your own lawyer or as section 6 allows;

(b) use it only to take part in the Process;

(c) not copy, photograph, record, screenshot or download it, or enter it into any AI tool or outside service, unless Eden gives written permission; and

(d) tell Eden promptly if you learn of any disclosure or use that breaks this agreement.

If a court or government body orders you to disclose Confidential Information, you may do so. Where the law allows, you will first give Eden prompt notice so it can seek protection, and you will disclose only what the order requires.

**4. Return or destroy.** When the Process ends, or within 5 business days of Eden's request, you will return or destroy all Confidential Information in your possession. You will confirm this in writing if Eden asks.

**5. No going around Eden.** For 12 months after the last day of the Process, you will not use Confidential Information, directly or through anyone else, to:

(a) solicit or accept recruiting business from any client or prospect Eden disclosed to you, except on Eden's behalf; or

(b) present, refer or place any candidate Eden disclosed to you, except on Eden's behalf.

Nothing in this agreement stops you from applying for, accepting or holding any job with any employer, including an Eden client, or from working in recruiting.

**6. What this agreement does not stop.** Nothing in this agreement prevents you from:

(a) reporting a possible violation of law to a government agency, including the National Labor Relations Board, the EEOC, the SEC or a state agency, or taking part in its investigation;

(b) discussing or disclosing wages, hours or other terms and conditions of employment, or any conduct you reasonably believe is unlawful, including discrimination, harassment, retaliation or sexual assault; or

(c) testifying truthfully in any legal proceeding.

**Defend Trade Secrets Act notice (18 U.S.C. 1833(b)).** You will not be held criminally or civilly liable under any federal or state trade secret law for disclosing a trade secret (a) in confidence to a federal, state or local government official, directly or indirectly, or to an attorney, solely for the purpose of reporting or investigating a suspected violation of law; or (b) in a complaint or other document filed under seal in a lawsuit or other proceeding. If you sue an employer for retaliation for reporting a suspected violation of law, you may disclose the trade secret to your attorney and use it in the court proceeding, if you file any document containing the trade secret under seal and do not disclose it except under court order.

**7. Your information.** Eden will keep confidential the personal information you give it in the Process, including your resume, pay history and references. Eden will use it only to evaluate you and as the law requires.

**8. No license; no warranty.** All Confidential Information remains Eden's property. This agreement gives you no license or other right in it, including in any copyright, trade secret or software. Eden provides it "as is" and is not required to disclose anything.

**9. Term.** Your duties under sections 3 and 4 last for 2 years after the last day of the Process. For any trade secret, they last for as long as it remains a trade secret under law. If you join Eden, this agreement continues until a signed employment or contractor agreement replaces it.

**10. Remedies.** A breach may cause Eden harm that money alone cannot fix. Eden may therefore seek an injunction, in addition to any other remedy, without posting a bond where the court allows. In any lawsuit to enforce this agreement, the winning party may recover its reasonable attorney's fees.

**11. Law and venue.** Idaho law governs this agreement, without regard to its conflict-of-law rules. Any lawsuit will be brought in the state or federal courts in Ada County, Idaho, unless the law where you live requires otherwise.

**12. General.** This is the entire agreement on its subject. Changes must be in writing and signed by both parties. If a court finds any part unenforceable, the court may narrow that part, and the rest stays in effect. Electronic signatures and signed copies count as originals.

## Drafting notes (not part of the agreement)

| Choice | Why | Grade |
|---|---|---|
| One-way, with section 7 on the candidate's own data | Eden is the one disclosing. A one-line promise about the candidate's resume and pay history makes it read as fair. | LIKELY |
| Non-use limits on section 5, with a carve-out for any job | Ties the restraint to Eden's information rather than to the candidate's livelihood. That keeps it out of noncompete territory under Idaho 44-2701, W.S. 1-23-108 and ESHB 1155. | LIKELY |
| No liquidated damages | Against an individual it risks being struck as a penalty, and it scares candidates off. Injunction and fee-shifting are the real remedies. | LIKELY |
| Section 6 carve-outs | Applicants are NLRA employees (*Phelps Dodge*, 1941). SEC Rule 21F-17 bars impeding whistleblowers. The Speak Out Act (42 U.S.C. 19401-19404) voids pre-dispute NDAs on sexual harassment claims. | LIKELY |
| DTSA notice | 18 U.S.C. 1833(b)(3) requires it only for "employees" and contractors, so it is not strictly required for a candidate. It is included because it costs nothing and keeps the form usable for anyone hired. | SETTLED (text read 2 Nov 2026) |
| E-signature | Federal E-SIGN Act (15 U.S.C. 7001) and Idaho's UETA (Idaho Code 28-50-107). | LIKELY |
