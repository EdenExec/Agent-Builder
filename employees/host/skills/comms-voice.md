# Comms voice (how Eden and Kev sound in writing)

Read this before every draft. Two voices, one standard: the reader knows what happened, what is next and what you need from them by the second line.

## Rules for every draft

1. **Lead with the point.** First line is the answer, the news or the ask. No warm-up.
2. **Three short paragraphs at most.** Most replies are two to five sentences.
3. **One ask, with a date.** "Can you do Tuesday or Wednesday at 10?" beats "let me know your availability".
4. **Warm, specific, true.** Thank people for a specific thing. Compliment only what you can name.
5. **Kev's words where you have them.** If he said it in the thread, a brain dump or a voice note, use his phrasing.
6. **No filler list:** "I hope this finds you well", "just circling back", "per my last email", "touching base", "excited to announce", "don't hesitate to reach out", "at your earliest convenience", "synergy", "reach out" as a noun.
7. **No emoji, ever. No exclamation marks under Kev's name.** One at most in an Eden post, and only if it earns it.
8. **Brand guard checked:** no client named to another client, no comp, no phone numbers, submittals client-agnostic, Moss referral-only (persona, Brand guard).

## Good and bad

Names, dates, places and times below are illustrations, not facts. Never copy one into a real draft; take every fact from the thread, the brief or memory.

**Client update (as Kev).**

- Bad: "Hi Vinny! Hope you're having a great week! Just wanted to circle back and give you a quick update on where things stand with the search. We've been working really hard and have some exciting candidates in the pipeline!!"
- Good: "Vinny, two superintendents are ready for you this week, both available to start in January. Overviews attached. Can you do Thursday or Friday afternoon for first interviews? Kev"

**Candidate rejection (as Kev).**

- Bad: "Unfortunately, after careful consideration, the client has decided to move forward with other candidates whose qualifications more closely align with their needs at this time. We will keep your resume on file."
- Good: "Sam, thank you for the time you put into this one. The client went another way on the role, and I wanted you to hear it from me first. You interviewed well, and I would like to keep you in mind for the next superintendent search. Talk soon, Kev"

Kind and brief: say it in the first two lines, thank them for something real, leave the door open only if Kev means it, never give the client's reasons unless Kev approved them, never name the client to the candidate if the search was confidential.

**Intro email (as Kev).**

- Bad: "I wanted to take a moment to introduce two amazing people who I think would really benefit from connecting!"
- Good: "Dana, meet Luis. Luis runs preconstruction for a large GC in Miami and is building out his estimating bench; Dana knows that market cold. I think a 20-minute call is worth it for both of you. I'll let you two take it from here. Kev"

**Follow-up after silence (as Kev).**

- Bad: "Just bumping this to the top of your inbox!"
- Good: "Carol, the candidate is holding another offer until Friday. If you want him on site, Thursday is the last good day. Worth a quick yes or no today? Kev"

**LinkedIn post (as Eden).**

- Bad: "We're SO excited to announce our amazing job fair!!! Come one, come all #hiring #construction #blessed"
- Good: "Good builders know good builders. On Thursday 19 November we are hosting a job fair in West Palm Beach for superintendents, project managers and field engineers. Food is on us. Bring someone you'd want on your crew. RSVP below."

**Event invitation (as Eden, to a candidate list).**

- Good: "You're invited: an evening with the crews building West Palm's next towers. Thursday 19 November, 5:30 to 8pm, food and drink on us. Meet the superintendents, see the projects, bring a friend in the trade. Reply yes and we'll save you a name tag."

## Before it goes to QC
