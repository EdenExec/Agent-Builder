## The three ideas

- **A Boston boy.** Born January 17, 1706, the fifteenth of seventeen children of Josiah, a candle and soap maker. School ended at about ten; he taught himself from books. Apprenticed at 12 to his brother James, a printer. At 16 he wrote as "Silence Dogood", disguised his hand and slipped the letters under the print-shop door at night.
- **The swimmer.** As a boy he made oval wooden hand paddles shaped like a painter's palette (faster, but they tired his wrists). He crossed a pond nearly a mile wide on his back, pulled by a kite, while another boy carried his clothes around. In London he swam the Thames from Chelsea to Blackfriars. International Swimming Hall of Fame, 1968.
- **Rolls and virtues.** Ran away to Philadelphia at 17 with a Dutch dollar and a shilling in copper; three puffy rolls, one under each arm; Deborah Read thought he looked ridiculous (he later married her); he gave two rolls to a mother and child. At about 20 he chose thirteen virtues, kept a little book with a black dot for each slip, one virtue a week. Humility was added after a Quaker friend said he was thought proud: "Imitate Jesus and Socrates." G's winning moves (silence, cleanliness, humility) come from this list.

## Ten questions for the truck

1. **Which child of how many was Ben?** Fifteenth of seventeen.
2. **What did his father make?** Candles and soap.
3. **What did Ben wear on his hands to swim faster?** Wooden paddles shaped like a painter's palette.
4. **How did he cross the pond?** On his back, pulled by a kite.
5. **Who was Silence Dogood?** Ben, writing as a widow, letters under the door at night.
6. **How old was he when he ran away to Philadelphia?** Seventeen.
7. **What did he buy with three pennies?** Three puffy rolls.
8. **What happened with Deborah Read?** She laughed at how he looked; later he married her.
9. **What was in Ben's little book?** Thirteen virtues, a black dot for every slip.
10. **Why did he add humility?** A Quaker friend told him people thought he was proud.
