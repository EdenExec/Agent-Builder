## The three ideas

- **Who and where.** The largest marsupial on Earth and the largest land mammal native to Australia. Hot, dry central and western Australia. Males (boomers) red-brown, up to about 90 kg (about 200 lb); females blue-grey, called blue flyers; babies are joeys.
- **Living springs.** Up to 8 to 9 m (26 to 30 ft) in one hop, about 60 km/h (37 mph). Leg tendons store and return energy, so going faster costs little extra. The tail balances the hop, props them up like a stool, and works like a fifth leg when grazing. Males box.
- **Babies and heat.** About 33 days of pregnancy; the joey is born jellybean-sized, blind and hairless, and crawls to the pouch alone. Head out at about six months, leaves the pouch for good around eight months, nurses to about a year. Mothers can pause a waiting embryo (embryonic diapause) until the pouch is free or food returns. They cool off in shade, by panting and by licking their forearms, and get much of their water from plants.

## Ten questions for the truck

1. **What's a marsupial?** A mammal that carries its baby in a pouch.
2. **What color are males and females?** Males red-brown (boomers), females blue-grey (blue flyers).
3. **How far can one hop go?** 8 to 9 meters, about 30 feet.
4. **How fast can they go?** About 60 km/h, around 37 mph.
5. **Why don't they get tired hopping?** Their tendons work like springs.
6. **What does the tail do?** Balance, a stool to lean on, and a fifth leg when walking slowly.
7. **How big is a newborn joey?** About the size of a jellybean.
8. **How does it get into the pouch?** It crawls up by itself.
9. **What's the pause button?** A mother can pause a waiting baby until the pouch is free.
10. **How do they cool off?** Shade, panting, and licking their arms.

## Try this

Measure your best standing jump with a tape measure, then measure 9 meters on the ground and stand at the end. That's one kangaroo hop.
