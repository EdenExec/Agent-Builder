## The three ideas

- **The lie.** A trout eats a fly that looks like food (size first, shape second, colour last) and moves like food (a drag-free drift). Three kinds in the box: dry flies float, nymphs sink, streamers swim like small fish.
- **The brown trout.** Butter-gold with black and red spots ringed in pale halos. Came from Europe: eggs from Germany in 1883, first planted in Michigan in 1884. Careful, likes a roof, feeds in low light, and big ones eat fish (streamers). Browns spawn in fall: stay off the redds.
- **The bull trout.** Really a char. Needs the coldest, cleanest water. In the Boise system, the Middle and North Forks winter in Arrowrock Reservoir and spawn in the headwaters. Threatened; Idaho allows no harvest anywhere. "No black, put it back."

## Who else is fishing

Osprey (dives feet first, carries the fish head-forward), great blue heron (stands still, then strikes), bald eagles in the cottonwoods in winter, river otters if you're lucky, and about 800 pairs of birds of prey nesting in the Snake River canyon south of Boise.

## Ten questions for the truck

1. **Why does a trout eat a fake fly?** It looks like food and moves like food, and the trout has a split second to decide.
2. **In what order does a trout judge a fly?** Size, then shape, then colour.
3. **What is drag?** The line pulling the fly so it skates across the water. Real bugs don't do that.
4. **Dry fly, nymph, streamer: which sinks?** The nymph. Dries float; streamers are pulled like a small fish.
5. **Where did brown trout come from?** Europe. Eggs came from Germany in 1883; the first went into a Michigan river in 1884.
6. **Where and when does a big brown feed?** Under a roof (cut bank, log), in low light: dawn, dusk, cloudy days, night.
7. **What is a redd, and what do you do when you see one?** A swept patch of clean gravel holding eggs. Walk around it.
8. **Is a bull trout really a trout?** No, it's a char, like a brook trout.
9. **How do you tell a bull trout from a brook trout?** The back fin. Brook trout have black wavy marks; bull trout have none. No black, put it back.
10. **Four ways to release a fish well?** Wet hands, keep it in the water, hold it gently away from the gills, face it upstream until it swims off.

## For Kev

- **Where to look for a bull trout:** the Middle Fork and North Fork Boise above Arrowrock Reservoir, in cold water, with the current Idaho Fish and Game rules for that exact stretch checked before the trip. Catch-and-release only, everywhere in Idaho. Barbless hooks help.
- **October:** browns are spawning. Pick spots away from shallow gravel, and teach G to read a redd.
- **The in-town Boise River** (Barber Dam to Lucky Peak) lists rainbow and brown trout, mountain whitefish, sculpins, suckers and bass in IDFG surveys.
