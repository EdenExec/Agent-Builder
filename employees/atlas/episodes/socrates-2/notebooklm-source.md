## Teaching points

- **The toughest man in the army.** As a hoplite at Potidaea (432 BC, aged about 37) he outlasted everyone on hunger and marched barefoot on ice (Alcibiades in Plato's Symposium). He once stood thinking from dawn to the next dawn.
- **The prize he gave away.** He saved the wounded Alcibiades and his armour, then was 'more eager than the generals' that Alcibiades, not he, get the prize of valour. At Delium (424 BC) he walked calmly in the rout with the general Laches and was left alone.
- **Quiet no, and the gadfly.** Under the Thirty (404 to 403 BC) he and four others were ordered to fetch Leon of Salamis to be killed; the four went, Socrates 'went quietly home' (Apology). He called himself a gadfly God gave the sleepy horse of Athens.

## Facts to keep straight

1. **What kind of soldier was Socrates?** A hoplite: a foot soldier with a round shield and spear.
2. **How did he march in the winter at Potidaea?** Barefoot on ice, in one cloak.
3. **How long did he stand thinking?** From one dawn to the next.
4. **Whose life did he save?** Alcibiades'.
5. **What did he do about the prize for bravery?** Pushed for Alcibiades to get it instead of him.
6. **What did he do in the retreat at Delium?** Walked calmly, so no one attacked him.
7. **Who were the Thirty?** A cruel group of thirty men who ruled Athens after the war.
8. **What did he do when they ordered him to arrest Leon?** Went home and refused to do wrong.
9. **What is a gadfly?** A biting fly that bothers horses; Socrates said he woke Athens up.
10. **Does humble mean weak or shy?** No: humble people can be the bravest, because they don't need the prize.
