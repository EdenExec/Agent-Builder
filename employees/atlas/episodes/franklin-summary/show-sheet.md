## The three ideas

- **The self-taught boy.** Boston 1706, 15th of 17; school ended at about 10; books; the kite-swim; Silence Dogood; runaway at 17; three puffy rolls and the two he gave away; thirteen virtues and the little book; humility added after a Quaker friend's word.
- **The doer and the scientist.** Printer, Poor Richard, library, fire company, the stove he would not patent; electricity, the 1752 kite and key, the lightning rod given away; bifocals, armonica, the Gulf Stream, chess.
- **The founder, humble to the end.** Join, or Die (1754); silent under insult in London; the Declaration at 70; the fur cap in France and the alliance; the Constitution at 81: the sparrow, and his closing speech, read by James Wilson: 'the older I grow, the more apt I am to doubt my own judgment', asking each member to 'doubt a little of his own infallibility'; the rising sun; 'a republic, if you can keep it'. Honest about slavery, and his change.

## Ten questions for the truck

1. **What did Ben spend his pennies on?** Books.
2. **Who was Silence Dogood?** Ben, writing as a widow.
3. **What did he do with his extra rolls?** Gave them to a mother and child.
4. **Why did he add humility?** A Quaker friend said people thought him proud.
5. **What did he refuse to patent?** His stove and the lightning rod.
6. **What did the kite and key prove?** Lightning is electricity.
7. **What did 'Join, or Die' mean?** The colonies were strong only together.
8. **What did he wear in France?** A plain fur cap.
9. **What did he ask the Convention to do on the last day?** Doubt a little of their own infallibility, and sign.
10. **Rising sun or setting sun?** Rising.
