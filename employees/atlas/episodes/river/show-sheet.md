# What you're fishing for: how to read a river

Atlas podcast, episode 2 for the Boise road trip. Thursday 8 October 2026. Guide: Atlas. Kid: Finn. Script 4,481 words, nine segments. Audio: river-episode-cloud.mp3, 30 min 34 s.

## The three ideas

- **The fish.** A trout wants four things: cold water with oxygen in it, food drifting past, a roof over its head, and as little current as it can manage while still eating. It faces into the current and lets the river bring dinner. Rainbows take the seams and riffle edges, cutthroat the same water, browns the dark undercut banks and pools.
- **The water.** A river repeats one pattern: riffle, run, pool, tailout. Inside it, five signs say where a fish holds: the seam (fast beside slow), the eddy, the foam line ("foam is home"), the undercut bank, and the pillow and pocket around a rock. Depth gives the roof, speed sets the work, structure makes the slow pocket next to the fast belt.
- **The bug.** Nine of ten trout meals are insects, most eaten underwater as nymphs before they hatch. Four families: mayflies, caddis, stoneflies, midges. A nymph sinks and copies the crawler; a dry floats and copies the hatched bug. Match the hatch by size first, shape second, colour last.

## Ten questions for the truck (answers for Kev)

1. **What four things does a trout want?** Cold water with oxygen, food drifting past, cover, and as little current as it can manage while still eating.
2. **Why does a trout face upstream?** The current runs properly through its gills and brings the food to it. Really it faces into the current; in an eddy that can be downstream.
3. **Name the four parts of a river in order.** Riffle, run, pool, tailout. Then it repeats.
4. **What is a seam?** The line where fast water runs beside slow water. The trout rests on the slow side with its nose on the line and eats off the fast side.
5. **"Foam is home." What does it mean?** Foam collects on the lane where the currents meet, the same lane the bugs drift down, so a foam line is the conveyor belt painted white.
6. **How do you tell a cutthroat from a rainbow from a brown?** Cutthroat: red or orange slash under the jaw. Rainbow: pink stripe, black spots, white tips on the belly fins. Brown: gold with black and red spots ringed in blue-grey.
7. **Nymph or dry fly?** A nymph sinks and copies the underwater stage; a dry floats and copies the hatched bug. Most meals are nymphs. Rings on the water mean dry; no rings, fish a nymph.
8. **What is hatching here in October?** Blue-winged olives in the afternoons, October caddis at dusk, midges always; cream crane flies on the South Fork.
9. **What is drag, and what is a dead drift?** Drag is the line pulling the fly faster or sideways than the water it sits in; it is the main reason a fish refuses. A dead drift is the fly riding at exactly the water's speed. Fix it with slack, a mend, and a shorter cast.
10. **Three October safety rules?** Nothing above the knees in fast water; shuffle, wader belt tight, cross at the tailout, feet downstream if you go in. Glasses and hat, look behind before every cast, barbless hooks. Tell someone where you are.
