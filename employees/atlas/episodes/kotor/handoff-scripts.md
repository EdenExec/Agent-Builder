# Kotor Old Town Walking Tour: Audio Scripts
For Kim and Dave, Wednesday 14 October 2026. Seven short tracks, one per stop, about 19 minutes in all.

## Production notes
- **Voice:** ElevenLabs "Chris" (iP95p4xoKVk53GoZ742B), eleven_multilingual_v2, same settings as Dubrovnik. Warm, curious, a friend showing them his favorite town. Direct address ("Kim, Dave").
- **Pacing:** [PAUSE] is a 1.5-second beat, [LONG PAUSE] three seconds, for looking around.
- **Port:** the trip guide and the Celebrity Ascent schedule (cruisetimetables.com) put the ship in **Bar**, 07:00 to 19:00, not Kotor. Kotor is about 60 km and 1¼ to 1½ hours by road from Bar. The scripts never assume the ship is at the pier, so they work however Kim and Dave reach the Sea Gate.
- **Pronunciation (spoken text only, see SAY in narration.py):** Kotor KOH-tor; Crna Gora TSER-nah GOH-rah; Cattaro kaht-TAH-roh; Tryphon TRY-fon; Boka BOH-kah; Grgurina GER-goo-ree-nah; Pima PEE-mah; Perast PEH-rahst; Martinović mar-TEE-no-vich; Kacafrangi kah-tsah-FRAHN-gee; Nemanja NEH-mahn-yah; kolo KOH-loh.

## Order of the walk
Sea Gate, then the Square of Arms and Clock Tower, past the Pima Palace on the Square of Flour to the Cathedral of St Tryphon, north to the Maritime Museum, St Luke's Square (St Luke and St Nicholas), and the River Gate. The climb to the Fortress of St John is optional and comes with a warning. The loop is about a third of a mile; stops are one to three minutes apart.

## TRACK 00: THE SEA GATE ("Under the Black Mountain"), 3:11
*Play on the quay outside the main gate, before walking in.*

Stop here for a moment, before you go through the gate. Turn around and look at the water. Then turn back and look up. Way up.

Kim, Dave, welcome to Kotor. [PAUSE] You're standing at the far inside corner of the Bay of Kotor, the Boka, as the locals call it. It looks like a Norwegian fjord, and people often call it one. But it isn't. A fjord is carved out by glaciers. This bay is an old river canyon that the sea flooded long ago. Either way, it's one of the most sheltered harbors in the Mediterranean, and that is exactly why people have lived here for more than two thousand years.

Now look at the gray wall of rock behind the town. Those are the mountains that gave this country its name. Montenegro means Black Mountain. In the local language it's Crna Gora. It's one of the youngest countries in Europe, independent only since 2006. And here's a fun one: they pay in euros, even though they're not in the European Union. [PAUSE]

See the walls zig-zagging up the cliff, all the way to the top? That's one long system of defenses, about four and a half kilometers of wall, nearly three miles, and in places it's up to sixty-five feet high. Most of what you see was built by Venice, which ruled Kotor for almost four hundred years, from 1420 to 1797. The Venetians called this town Cattaro, and you'll still see that name on hotels and shops.

Kotor is small, but history has knocked it around. The Ottoman Turks laid siege to it in 1538 and again in 1657, and the walls held. Earthquakes were harder to stop. A great one in 1667 did terrible damage, and another, on the fifteenth of April 1979, damaged a huge share of the old buildings around this bay. That same year UNESCO made the region a World Heritage Site, and it stayed on UNESCO's danger list until 2003, while the town was patiently put back together. [LONG PAUSE]

Now, the gate. This is the Sea Gate, also called the Main Gate, built in 1555. Over the arch you'll see a date: the twenty-first of November, 1944. That's the day Kotor was freed at the end of the Second World War. The words beside it are a wartime saying linked to Marshal Tito, the leader of old Yugoslavia. They mean: "We don't want what belongs to others, and we will never give up what is ours." [PAUSE] And see if you can spot a weathered stone lion with wings on the wall nearby. That's the lion of Saint Mark, the badge of Venice. They put him everywhere they ruled.

Your action: walk through the gate slowly. Just inside, look for a stone carving from the 1400s: the Virgin Mary with the Christ child, and a saint on each side. One of them is Saint Tryphon, the patron saint of Kotor. Remember his name. You'll meet him again. When you come out into the big open square, stop, and press play on track one.

## TRACK 01: THE SQUARE OF ARMS AND THE CLOCK TOWER ("The tower that leans"), 2:47
*Play in the big square just inside the gate, facing the clock tower.*

This is the Square of Arms, the biggest square in Kotor. The name goes back to the Venetian days, when weapons and gunpowder were made and stored right here. [PAUSE] Look around. Almost every building on this square once had a job to do for the city.

The tall stone tower with the clock, right in front of you, is the City Clock Tower. Work on it began in 1602. Now look at it carefully. Does it look a little bit crooked? It is. The earthquakes of 1667 and 1979 both gave it a shove, and today it leans just slightly. Engineers have strengthened its foundations, and the town has decided to leave it the way it is. A tower with a bit of a limp, still keeping time.

At its foot is a small stone pyramid. That's a pillory, a copy of the medieval one. If you broke the rules in old Kotor, you could be chained to it, right here in front of the whole town, so everybody knew what you'd done. [PAUSE] Public shame was the punishment. It's also a very good spot for a picture of Dave. [PAUSE]

Now look to your left, along the city wall. The long building there was the Rector's Palace, where the governor sent from Venice lived and ran the town. Just past it is a building that became a theater in 1810. That was during Napoleon's time, when the French held Kotor for a few years. It was one of the very first theaters in the Balkans. Today both buildings are part of the Hotel Cattaro. Somewhere on this square, too, is the old Arsenal, where Venice kept its guns.

The French didn't stay long. In the winter of 1813, British sailors did something nobody thought they could. They hauled heavy cannons up onto the heights above town, and on the fifth of January 1814, the French garrison gave up. [LONG PAUSE]

One more thing before you go. Kotor's lanes twist and turn like a maze. People here like to say it was built that way on purpose, to confuse invaders. Don't worry about getting lost. The whole town is only a few hundred yards across, and if you head toward the water, you'll always find the walls.

Your action: walk past the clock tower on its right-hand side and keep going into the lanes beyond. In a minute or so you'll pass a small square with an elegant palace with stone balconies. That's the Pima Palace, on the Square of Flour. The squares here are named for what was sold on them. Keep going, following the little signs for the Cathedral. Press play on track two when you're standing in front of two tall bell towers.

## TRACK 02: THE CATHEDRAL OF SAINT TRYPHON ("A saint who came by sea"), 2:40
*Play on the cathedral square, facing the two bell towers.*

Here he is again: Saint Tryphon. This is his cathedral, and it is the pride of Kotor.

Who was he? [PAUSE] Tryphon was a young man from what is now Turkey, who as a boy looked after geese. He was a Christian at a dangerous time, and around the year 250 he was put to death for his faith in the city of Nicaea. So how did a goose boy from Asia Minor become the patron saint of a little town on the Adriatic?

Here's the story. In the year 809, Venetian merchants arrived in this bay carrying his relics. A wealthy citizen of Kotor named Andrea Saracenis bought them, and built a church to keep them safe. Kotor has belonged to Saint Tryphon ever since. [PAUSE]

The building in front of you went up on that spot, and it was consecrated on the nineteenth of June, 1166. That's around the same time they were starting work on Notre-Dame in Paris. It's built in the Romanesque style: thick walls, round arches, solid and calm.

Now look at the two bell towers. Notice anything? They don't match. The earthquake of 1667 brought down the front of the church and both towers. Kotor rebuilt them, but the money ran out, and the tower on the left was never quite finished. To this day it's a little shorter than its twin. [PAUSE] I like that. It's honest. It tells you this town did the best it could with what it had.

Then came the earthquake of 1979, which badly damaged the cathedral again. The repairs took fourteen years, and the church was blessed again in the year 2000.

Step inside, for a small ticket. Look up for the fragments of wall paintings from the 1300s, still clinging to the stone. And go upstairs to the relic chapel. Behind the silver and gold is what this whole church was built for: the relics of Saint Tryphon, including his head. [LONG PAUSE]

Every winter, from the thirteenth of January to Saint Tryphon's Day on the third of February, Kotor throws its biggest festival, with processions through the town led by men in old sailors' uniforms. They're called the Boka Navy, and you'll meet them at the next stop.

Your action: go in, take a seat in a pew, and rest your legs for a minute. It's cool inside. When you're ready, come back out, stand facing the cathedral, and take the lane on your left. Walk north for about a minute, following the signs for the Maritime Museum. Press play on track three when you reach a small square with a grand palace on it.

## TRACK 03: THE MARITIME MUSEUM AND THE BOKA NAVY ("Sailors since 809"), 2:46
*Play in front of the Grgurina Palace (Maritime Museum).*

Welcome to the sailors' corner of Kotor. The handsome building in front of you is the Grgurina Palace, built in the 1700s for a well-off Kotor family. Since 1938 it has been the Maritime Museum of Montenegro. [PAUSE]

If you want to understand Kotor, you have to understand that this was a town of sailors. The whole Bay of Kotor was. Its captains carried cargo across the Mediterranean and beyond, and for centuries the men of the Boka were some of the best seamen on the Adriatic.

And they've been organized for a very long time. The Boka Navy is a brotherhood of sailors that traces its beginnings to the year 809, when Saint Tryphon's relics arrived in town. That's more than twelve hundred years. [PAUSE] It started as a real working fraternity of seamen. Today it's a ceremonial company, men, women and children, who march in old-fashioned uniforms, carry antique weapons, and dance a slow circle dance called the kolo in front of the cathedral on the saint's feast. In 2021 UNESCO added the Boka Navy to its list of the world's living heritage. Not bad for a club that started before William the Conqueror was born.

Here's another story about how good these sailors were. Just up the bay is a little town called Perast. Around 1698, a sea captain there named Marko Martinović ran a school of navigation, and Peter the Great, the Tsar of Russia, sent young Russian noblemen all the way here to learn how to sail. [PAUSE] Russia's future navy took some of its first lessons from the captains of this bay.

The museum itself began around 1880 as the sailors' own collection, and it opened to the public in 1900. Inside you'll find beautiful ship models, captains' portraits, uniforms, and weapons from the long fight against pirates. If you like ships at all, it's worth half an hour and a small ticket. [LONG PAUSE]

Before you go, look at the stonework of the grand houses around you. Every one of them tells the same story: money came from the sea, and the families who made it wanted the whole town to know it.

Your action: if the museum appeals, go in. If not, simply walk a few steps north, around the corner, to Saint Luke's Square. You're looking for a small, low stone church, with a much bigger church right beside it. Press play on track four when you're in front of the little one.

## TRACK 04: SAINT LUKE'S CHURCH AND SAINT NICHOLAS ("One roof, two altars"), 2:44
*Play on St Luke's Square, in front of the small stone church.*

Look at this little church. It's not grand. It's not tall. But it might be the most remarkable building in Kotor.

This is the Church of Saint Luke, built in 1195. That makes it more than eight hundred years old. The man who paid for it, Mauro Kacafrangi, had his name carved over the door on the front. In those days Kotor belonged to the medieval Serbian state of Stefan Nemanja, and Saint Luke's was built as a Catholic church. [PAUSE]

And here's why it's special. From 1657 to 1812, this church held two altars, one Catholic and one Orthodox. For more than a hundred and fifty years, two different Christian communities worshipped under this one small roof. [PAUSE] In 1812 it was handed over to the Orthodox Church, and it's still Orthodox today.

In a part of the world that has known more than its share of division, that's worth a quiet minute. Two families of believers, sharing one house of God, for a century and a half.

There's more. Kotor's earthquakes knocked down towers, palaces and the front of a cathedral. People here will tell you that this little church is the one building in town that came through all of them without major damage. [PAUSE] And if you step inside, look down. The floor is made of gravestones. People were buried under this floor until the 1930s.

Now look at the big church beside it, with the two bell towers and the black domes. That's the Orthodox Church of Saint Nicholas. It's young by Kotor standards. An older church on this spot burned down on Christmas Eve, 1896, and this one was built between 1902 and 1909. Saint Nicholas is the patron saint of sailors, which makes perfect sense in a town like this one. If the doors are open, step in. It's dim, full of icons and candlelight, and very peaceful. Please dress modestly and keep your voices low. [LONG PAUSE]

So in one small square you have an eight-hundred-year-old church that two faiths shared, and a newer one built after a fire. Old and new, side by side, which is pretty much the story of Kotor.

Your action: take a look inside either church if you can. Then head on toward the northern edge of town. Listen for rushing water. When you reach the gate in the wall beside the river, press play on track five.

## TRACK 05: THE RIVER GATE AND THE CATS OF KOTOR ("Cats, a river and the north gate"), 2:16
*Play at the River Gate (North Gate).*

Hear that water? That's the little river that runs along the north side of the walls. This is the River Gate, also called the North Gate, built in 1540. Beside it is the Bembo Bastion, from the same year, a great stone gun platform built to hold the cannons that guarded this side of the town. Today it's used as an open-air theater, so the only thing it fires these days is music.

Look at the gate itself. On the outer side you'll find the emblems of Venice carved in stone, including, once again, the winged lion of Saint Mark. [PAUSE] Between the river on this side, the sea on the other, and the mountain behind, Kotor was a very hard nut to crack.

Now, you've probably noticed some other residents of this town by now. The cats. [PAUSE] Kotor has a lot of cats. They sleep on the warm stone, on the café chairs, on the church steps, and they act like they own the place, which, honestly, they kind of do. The cats have become a symbol of the city. There are cat shops, cat souvenirs, and even a little Cats Museum, tucked into a lane near the Square of Arms.

How did a whole town end up full of cats? The story people tell is that sailors brought them home on their ships, to keep the rats down, and the cats simply moved in. [PAUSE] Whether or not that's the whole truth, Kotor is proud of them. Locals feed them and look after them. Feel free to say hello, but please don't pick them up. They're not pets. They're citizens.

One more thing to look for. Up on the mountain above you, you can see the walls climbing toward the very top. That's the Fortress of Saint John, and the path that leads up to it starts close by. Next to this gate, near the foot of the cliff, there's an entrance to the climb, with a ticket booth.

Kim, Dave, that's the end of the walk in town. [LONG PAUSE] The next track is about the climb. It's optional, and I want you to hear it before you decide whether to try it. Press play on track six when you're ready, or find a bench in the shade and listen there.

## TRACK 06: THE FORTRESS OF SAINT JOHN (OPTIONAL) AND FAREWELL ("Above the bay"), 2:36
*Play before deciding on the climb; a bench in the shade is fine.*

Okay. Let's talk honestly about the climb.

The Fortress of Saint John sits close to nine hundred feet above the sea. To get there, you climb roughly thirteen hundred steps, plus some steep ramps. The steps are uneven, worn smooth and slippery in places, and there's very little shade. Even on a mild October day, that limestone soaks up the sun and throws it back at you. A fit walker needs well over an hour to get up and back down, and it's fine to take a lot longer. [PAUSE]

So here's my advice. If it's hot, if your knees are complaining, or if you're watching the clock for the ride back to the ship, skip it. You've already seen the best of Kotor. Nobody will think less of you. [PAUSE]

If you do want to try, there's a good halfway answer. Partway up is a little church called Our Lady of Remedy, built in 1518. Climb to that church and turn around. You'll get a beautiful view over the rooftops of the old town and the bay, without the whole climb. Bring water, wear shoes with good grip, take it slowly, and come down even more slowly. Going down is where people slip.

And if you go all the way to the top, you'll be standing where soldiers have kept watch for about fifteen hundred years. The Byzantine emperor Justinian rebuilt a fortress up there in the 500s, and Venice made it what you see now. Up there, you'll understand why everyone wanted this place: the whole bay laid out below you like a map. [LONG PAUSE]

Kim and Dave, that's our walk. You've stood under a gate that remembers a war, seen a clock tower with a limp, met a saint who arrived by sea, heard about sailors who taught a tsar's navy, stood in a church that two faiths shared, and said hello to the cats who run the place.

There's an old line about the wise man who built his house upon the rock. Kotor took that literally. It sits at the foot of a mountain, it's been shaken and knocked down more than once, and every time, it built again on the same foundation. Not a bad thing to carry home.

Your action: to get back to the Sea Gate, walk back through the town, or go out through the River Gate and follow the walls down to the water and along to the main gate. It's about five minutes. Keep a close eye on the time; remember the drive back to the ship. Before you go, find a spot with the mountain behind you, take a picture together, and send it to the family. [PAUSE] And treat yourselves to something cold. You've earned it.
