# How a house gets built: from a bare lot to the keys

**Atlas podcast, episode 1.** Boise road trip, Thursday 8 October 2026. Guide: Atlas. Kid: Finn. Audio: house-episode-cloud.mp3, 29 min 55 s (ffprobe 1795.2 s). Script: 4,493 spoken words, 145 turns, 7 segments.

## The three ideas

**1. The lot and the ground**
- The survey says where the house goes, the soil test says what it sits on, the paint lines say where the pipes run. Nobody digs before all three.
- A house presses on the dirt for a century. Soft or swelling ground sinks unevenly and the house cracks. Pisa: soft ground, 5.5 degrees of lean, fixed in 2001 by siphoning earth from under the high side.
- Footings sit below the frost line (about 24 inches around Boise) because freezing ground lifts a house like a jack. The foundation is the one part you cannot swap.

**2. The frame and the systems**
- The load path: every pound is a bucket passed from roof to rafters to walls to floor to foundation to dirt. A header carries weight around the hole a window makes.
- Rough-in order: plumber (big pipes, drains must run downhill), then electrician, then ducts, then the inspector, then insulation and drywall. A wall is open for about one week of its life.
- Brunelleschi's dome (1420 to 1436): no wooden framework, herringbone brick that locks each ring to the one below, stone ribs to carry the load. The order of the work is the craft.

**3. The skin and the finish**
- The envelope has one job: keep water out, and let what gets in get back out. Layers: roof and siding deflect, house wrap drains, insulation dries.
- Flashing's rule is a shingle's rule: everything higher laps over everything lower. Leaks cluster at windows, decks and penetrations. The ground must fall 6 inches in the first 10 feet.
- Wingspread, 1939: "Why don't you move your chair?" Fifty years of leaks and a $2.85 million repair. Pretty does not mean dry; countertops are the last one percent.

## Friday walk-through checklist (G outside first, then inside with Kev)

| Check | What to look for | Who |
|---|---|---|
| Grading | Ground falls away from every wall, about 6 in over 10 ft (a soda can over a pickup). No bowls under downspouts. | G |
| Foundation cracks | Thin, straight, vertical: usually shrinkage. Horizontal, stair-step, wider than a pencil, or one side higher: call Kev. | G |
| Doors and window corners | Every interior door latches; no wedge-shaped gaps. Diagonal drywall cracks at many windows is a pattern. | G |
| Window flashing | A metal lip over the top of each window frame outside. A thick caulk bead across the top is a repaired leak. | G |
| Water stains | Musty smell in basement or crawl space; brown rings on ceilings; a tide line on lower walls. | both |
| Attic ventilation | Slotted soffit panels low, ridge or roof vents high. Code: 1 sq ft of vent per 150 sq ft of attic (1/300 if balanced high and low). | G |
| Electrical panel | Open the door only, never the cover. Labeled breakers; no rust, scorch marks or burning smell. | Kev |
| Water heater age | Date sticker or serial number. Typical life 6 to 12 years. | Kev |
| Roof age | Curling edges, bare patches, moss. 3-tab shingles about 20 years; architectural about 30. | G |

Next step if anything is horizontal, stepped or wet: a licensed home inspector, and a structural engineer for the foundation. That is a professional's job, not a guess.

## Ten interview questions for the truck

1. **Why does the Leaning Tower of Pisa lean?** The ground under it is soft river mud, and the south side was softer, so it sank more. The lean started before the third floor was done.
2. **How did engineers tip it back?** They could not push it. They sucked small amounts of earth from under the high north side so it settled to match, moving the top back about 17 inches. Reopened 2001.
3. **What three things does a builder learn about a lot before digging?** Where it is (survey stakes), what the dirt is (soil test), and where the utilities run (red, blue, green, yellow paint).
4. **Why do footings go below the frost line?** Water in the soil freezes and expands, lifting the house, then drops it in spring. Below the frost line, about two feet in Boise, the ground does not freeze.
5. **What is the load path?** The route every pound travels: roof, rafters, walls, floor, foundation, dirt. If any hand in the line drops the bucket, something cracks or sags.
6. **Why is there a beam above a window?** A window is a hole in the wall. The header catches the weight from above and hands it to the studs on either side.
7. **Why does the plumber go before the electrician?** Drain pipes are big and must run downhill the whole way, so they pick their path first. Wires are skinny and bend around anything.
8. **What is flashing, and what is its rule?** Bent metal or tape at every hole in the skin. Everything higher laps over everything lower, so water goes out, not in.
9. **Which cracks matter?** Thin vertical ones are concrete shrinking. Horizontal, stair-step, wide or offset cracks, especially with sticking doors, mean the ground is moving.
10. **What did the Empire State builders do that Brunelleschi also did?** Planned the order and did each day's work all the way: 102 stories in one year and 45 days, about 4.5 stories a week.
