## The three ideas

- **The army.** Light square on the right, queen on her color, white moves first. Six ways to move: rook straight, bishop diagonal (stays on its color), queen both, king one step, knight an L that jumps and lands on the other color, pawn forward one (two on its first move), captures diagonally, promotes on the last row. Points: pawn 1, knight 3, bishop 3, rook 5, queen 9; the king is the game.
- **The goal.** You win by checkmate, not by taking pieces. Check: get out by moving, blocking or taking. Checkmate: no way out. Stalemate (no legal move and not in check) is a draw. Castling tucks the king away; en passant exists, learn it later.
- **The question.** Before every move: what does my opponent want? Then checks, captures, threats. In the opening: a pawn in the middle, knights and bishops out, castle.

## The masters

- **Paul Morphy**, New Orleans. The Opera Game, Paris, 1858: Morphy alone against the Duke of Brunswick and Count Isouard, mate in 17 by bringing every piece into the fight.
- **José Raúl Capablanca**, Havana. By his own account, he learned at about four by watching his father, spotted an illegal move, and later became world champion (1921 to 1927).

## Ten questions for the truck

1. **Which corner gets the light square?** The bottom right. Light on the right.
2. **Where does the queen start?** On her own color, next to the king.
3. **Which piece can jump?** Only the knight. It moves in an L and always lands on the other color.
4. **How does a pawn capture?** One square diagonally forward. It moves straight but attacks on a slant.
5. **What happens when a pawn reaches the far side?** It's promoted, usually to a queen.
6. **Two knights or one rook?** Two knights: 3 + 3 = 6 against 5.
7. **Your king is in check. What are the three ways out?** Move it, block it, take the attacker.
8. **What's the difference between checkmate and stalemate?** Checkmate: in check with no way out, you lose. Stalemate: not in check but no legal move, it's a draw.
9. **What does "checkmate" come from?** Persian "shah mat", roughly "the king is helpless".
10. **What's the one question before every move?** What does my opponent want? Then checks, captures, threats.
