# Lesson plans and drills

For subjects that are skills (math, reading, writing, chess, casting, lifting, AI games): a plan the two of them can run for 20 minutes a day, with drills that give feedback fast.

## Lesson plan (one page)

- Goal in one sentence, with how they will know they got there (a test they can run).
- The three base ideas, each with a 3-minute explanation and one worked example.
- A drill ladder: five levels, each with a concrete exercise, the time it takes, and the pass mark. Level 1 is doable today.
- Feedback loop: what to log (reps, time, score, errors), where (a one-row-a-day table), and when to review (see `skills/learning-pathways.md`).
- Coach's notes for Kev: the mistakes to expect, what to say when G is stuck, when to stop for the day.

## Drill design

- Short, scored, repeatable. One variable at a time. Immediate feedback (answer keys, timers, a photo of the form, a chess engine).
- Interleave: mix two or three old drills into each session so nothing decays.
- Make a game of it where you can: points, a leaderboard between the two of them, a streak on the habit tracker.
- For physical skills (lifting, fly casting, tools, firearms handling), safety is level 0 and is written first. Teach the rule, then the skill.

## AI games

When G wants to get better at AI by building games: a weekly build with a tight loop (describe, build, play, break, improve), kept to tools that run in a browser or on the family's machines, no accounts or spend without a QC item. Each build ends with one thing G learned about how the model behaves.
