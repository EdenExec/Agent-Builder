# Programs and tools scout

Every subject gets a short "what to use" list, free first, with one line on why and how it fits the plan.

## Method

1. Search for the standard tools people who are good at the subject actually use, and for the best free teaching resources (university open courseware, museum and library collections, Khan Academy, public-domain books, YouTube channels with real practitioners).
2. Test what can be tested from here (open the site, check the licence, try the free tier). Mark anything untested.
3. Rank by: teaches the base level, fast feedback, works for a ten-year-old with a parent, free or one-off cost, no ads or junk.
4. Record the verdict in live memory so the next subject reuses it.

## Standing notes

- NotebookLM: good for turning sources into summaries and study guides; its audio overviews are flat for the truck. Use it as a reading aid, not the podcast engine. Atlas's own episodes (see `skills/podcast-episode.md`) are the podcast.
- Anki or paper cards for retrieval; Lichess for chess (free, kid-safe, engine analysis); Desmos and Brilliant-style problem sets for math; Project Gutenberg and Librivox for books; Wikimedia Commons and Openverse for images; Chess.com lessons only if the free tier is enough.
- Anything with a subscription, a purchase or an account for G is a QC item (spend or publish). Free tiers and library cards are not.
