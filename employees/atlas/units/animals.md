# Unit: Animals of the world (G's pick, 11 October 2026)

G asked for animals: kangaroos, axolotls, lions, bears, fish, jellyfish, the animals around Boise, and a way to get dragonflies (with frogs for backup) to eat the mosquitoes on the ranch one day. Folder on the Desk: `animals`. Every lesson is pinned on the Map.

## The three ideas the whole unit hangs on

1. **Every animal is built for its place.** Habitat shapes the body: a kangaroo's legs for open desert, an axolotl's gills for a cold lake, a bull trout's need for snowmelt-cold water.
2. **Everything eats and is eaten.** The food web: bugs, trout, osprey; mosquito larvae, dragonfly nymphs, frogs. Change one strand and the rest moves.
3. **We are keepers.** "The Lord God took the man and put him in the Garden of Eden to work it and take care of it" (Genesis 2:15). Catch and release, staying off a redd, a pond with no poison in it.

## The ranch pond plan (for lesson 3; Kev decides if and when)

- **Dragonflies are the main patrol.** Their young (nymphs) live in water and eat mosquito larvae; the adults catch mosquitoes in the air.
- **They need still, fishless water.** Fish such as goldfish and koi eat dragonfly eggs and young nymphs. A pond of at least about 40 square feet, with one part about 2 feet deep so nymphs can last the winter.
- **Plants in and around it.** Underwater plants and leaf litter for nymphs to hide in; tall stems for nymphs to climb out on when they become adults.
- **No sprays.** Mosquito fogging and broad-spectrum insecticides kill dragonflies and their nymphs too.
- **Empty the temporary water.** Mosquitoes breed in water that stands for a week or two: old tires, buckets, culverts. Tip them out every week.
- **Frogs are backup, honestly.** Adult frogs eat insects, mosquitoes among them, but most frog food is other bugs, and tadpoles mostly eat algae. They help; dragonflies do more.
- **Adult layer for Kev:** a pond needs a plan for G's safety around water, and any pond or water feature on the ranch should be checked against local rules before digging.
