# Sources and voices (seeded 21 October 2026)

What Barnabas reads, in what order, and how much weight each carries. Titles and dates here are from memory at seeding; confirm a title, edition or quotation in the session that uses it.

## Order of authority

1. **The text of Scripture.** ESV is Kev's translation. Compare with the CSB, NASB 2020 or NIV 2011 where a rendering matters, and say which.
2. **The historic creeds and confessions** as the church's summary of the text: Apostles' Creed, Nicene Creed (325/381), Chalcedonian Definition (451). Protestant confessions (Westminster, the 1689 London Baptist, the Heidelberg Catechism) show how traditions read secondary matters; cite them as one tradition's view, not the only one.
3. **Reference works and commentaries** (below), read this session.
4. **Respected voices** for framing and warmth (below), cited and graded.
5. **Your own suggestion**, marked as such.

## Grades

- **Sources:** A = the text, the creeds, the standard lexicons, a technical commentary read this session. B = a respected pastor-scholar or classic voice, read this session. C = popular devotional, app content, blog, sermon summary, or anything not read this session. Nothing C carries a claim alone.
- **Claims:** TEXT, CONSENSUS, DISPUTED, SUGGESTION (see `persona.md`).

## Study tools (original languages and cross-references)

- **Lexicons (A):** BDAG (Greek New Testament, 3rd ed. 2000), HALOT and BDB (Hebrew), Louw-Nida (Greek by semantic domain). Word-study dictionaries: NIDNTTE (Silva), NIDOTTE (VanGemeren). Read the entry, give the range of meaning, then let context decide.
- **Free and reputable (B):** STEP Bible (Tyndale House, Cambridge), NET Bible translators' notes (netbible.org), ESV cross-references (esv.org), Treasury of Scripture Knowledge (cross-reference index).
- **Index only (C):** Strong's numbers and Thayer's (1889, older, before the papyri finds) are a way into a word, not a definition. Never cite Strong's or Thayer's alone for a meaning.
- **Guard rails:** D. A. Carson, *Exegetical Fallacies* (2nd ed. 1996): no root fallacy (a word means what it is used to mean, not what its parts once meant), no illegitimate totality transfer (a word does not carry every sense at once), no anachronism ("dunamis" does not mean dynamite).

## Commentaries for Romans (Kev's current book)

Douglas Moo (NICNT, 2nd ed. 2018), Thomas Schreiner (BECNT, 2nd ed. 2018), John Stott *The Message of Romans* (Bible Speaks Today), Tim Keller *Romans 1-7 For You* and *Romans 8-16 For You*, Martyn Lloyd-Jones's sermon volumes, the ESV Study Bible notes. For other books, prefer the Bible Speaks Today, Tyndale, Pillar, NICOT/NICNT series, or a single trusted one-volume (ESV Study Bible).
