# Study guide: one passage, studied well

Inductive method: observation (what does it say), interpretation (what did it mean to them, what does it mean), application (what does it ask of me). Text first, tools second, voices third. If the `anthropic-skills:bible-study-guide` skill is available, use it for the layout; this playbook sets the standards.

## Steps

1. **Fix the passage.** A paragraph or a chapter, never a verse alone. Read the paragraph before and after. Link the ESV text.
2. **Setting.** Author, audience, date range, occasion, genre (letter, narrative, poetry, wisdom, prophecy, apocalyptic), and where the passage sits in the book's argument. Five lines.
3. **Observe.** Repeated words, connectors ("therefore", "for", "but", "so that"), commands, promises, contrasts, lists, questions, the main verbs and who does them. Ask Kev to mark them with a pen before you show yours.
4. **Word notes, carefully.** Two or three words at most, chosen because the meaning turns on them. For each: the Greek or Hebrew word (transliterated), its range of meaning from a reputable lexicon read this session (BDAG, HALOT, BDB, Louw-Nida; free: STEP Bible, NET notes), and what context selects. Note wordplay, repetition or a word that links to another passage (for example *tōm*, integrity, in Psalm 25:21 and 78:72). No root fallacy, no "the Greek really means" overreach. Grade each note.
5. **Cross-references.** Three to five, each with one line on why it bears (ESV cross-references, Treasury of Scripture Knowledge). Prefer the same author first, then the same theme, then the Old Testament background, then fulfilment in Christ.
6. **Interpret.** The passage's main point in one sentence. Where faithful readers differ, give the main readings fairly (`memory/seed/doctrine-map.md`) and point to the pastor. One commentary view, named and graded.
7. **Christ and the gospel.** How the passage points to Christ or rests on the gospel, without forcing it.
8. **Apply.** Three questions: what does this show me about God; what does it show me about me; what is one step this week. Link to a Seven Pillar only where the text truly bears on it (`memory/seed/seven-pillars-in-scripture.md`); never bend a passage to fit a pillar.
9. **Respond.** A short prayer drawn from the passage, a memory verse, one journaling question for 5:30.

## Output

A one- to three-page guide in that order, pen-in-hand spacing, KDTW Group (`org: kdtw`), with sources at the foot (text links, lexicon entries, commentary pages, each graded). A Romans guide can go to Radar for the Sunday Edition deep dive; a guide on a personal struggle stays with Kev.

## Quality checks before handing over

- Every Scripture quotation checked against esv.org this session, or marked "reference only".
- Every word note names its lexicon. Every quotation from a voice has a source; otherwise "paraphrase".
- Nothing DISPUTED is stated as settled. No private content in a guide that leaves the conversation.
