Owner: Evan watches the inflow. Weekly reminder: Radar sets it (see handoff). Kev's concern: casting a wide net across many cities may spread the posts too thin.

## What Evan checks, per job (daily glance, weekly write-up)

Counts only, read from the LinkedIn Recruiter applicant list and the Job post stats:
1. Views, Easy Apply starts, applicants (week over week).
2. Applicants who pass the must-have screening questions (the number that matters, not total applicants).
3. Applicants by city and by level (for QC: Director, Manager, Coordinator).
4. Qualified applicants who are actually in or near the target city.
5. Applicants that are obviously off-target (use the PE ad as the warning sign: 38 applicants, about 9 not a fit and 10 students).

A row per job per week, in the same sheet, so trends show.

## Weekly tuning levers (Evan picks one or two per job, writes down what he changed)

- **Split the wide posts.** If one city or role gets nearly all the qualified applicants, split that city out into its own post and pause or narrow the rest. VDC (Miami, West Palm Beach, Reston, remote) and Traveling Superintendent are the first candidates.
- **Retitle.** Match the title to what candidates search: "Superintendent, Data Center" vs "Traveling Superintendent"; "QC Manager" vs "Quality Control Manager".
- **Rewrite the first three lines** (they show in the feed). Lead with the location and the project story.
- **Tighten or loosen screening.** If qualified applicants are under about 5 a week, loosen one must-have; if over half the applicants are off-target, tighten one.
- **Boost or pause.** Promote the job that has good conversion and low reach; pause the one that is all noise.
- **Pair with outbound.** For any job below target, hand Scout the title and city and Scout builds the call list (ZoomInfo and LinkedIn) so Nick and the recruiters source directly instead of waiting on applicants.

## Decision rule (proposed, for Kev to confirm)

At the end of week 2, any job with fewer than a set number of qualified in-market applicants gets split, retitled or moved to outbound only. Kev sets the number; none is assumed here.
