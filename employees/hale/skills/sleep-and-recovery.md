# Sleep and recovery

Sleep is the base of the whole plan: training, mood, appetite and patience with children all run on it. Kev's day starts at 5:30, so his night has to start early enough to give him seven hours or more. Read `memory/seed/guideline-starting-map.md` (Sleep) first.

## Targets (graded)

| Who | Target | Grade |
|---|---|---|
| Kev | 7 or more hours, at a regular time, seven nights a week | SETTLED (AASM) |
| Children | 9 to 12 hours at 6 to 12 years; 8 to 10 at 13 to 18; younger per the seed table | SETTLED (AASM, AAP) |
| Everyone | A steady wake time matters as much as total hours | LIKELY |

Working back from a 5:30 wake: lights out by about 10:00 for 7 hours of sleep with time to fall asleep, so the wind-down starts around 9:15. Show this arithmetic on his plan; mark the times "(A)" until he confirms them.

## The wind-down (pick three, not ten)

- Screens off 60 minutes before lights out; the phone charges outside the bedroom (AAP advice for families; LIKELY for adults).
- Caffeine done by about six hours before bed (LIKELY, Drake 2013); alcohol in the evening worsens sleep quality (LIKELY).
- The same last steps every night: dim lights, the next day stood up (Radar's Stand Up Tomorrow already does this), a few minutes of Scripture or prayer if Kev wants it there, the room cool and dark.
- Late hard training ends at least two hours before bed if it keeps him up (UNSURE; individual).

## Tracking that helps rather than worries

1. Use only what Kev already owns (a watch, a ring, a phone) or a two-line log: lights out, wake, how rested on 1 to 5. Buying a tracker is a `spend` QC item.
2. Look at the seven-day average, never one night. Device sleep stages and "readiness" scores are UNSURE: useful for trends in hours and regularity, not as a verdict. If tracking makes sleep worse (orthosomnia), stop tracking and keep the routine.
3. The weekly review (in `skills/habits-and-tracking.md`) carries one sleep line: average hours, bedtime regularity, one change.

## When it is a doctor question

Loud snoring with pauses or gasping, waking unrefreshed despite enough hours, falling asleep while driving, insomnia most nights for three months, restless legs, or a child who snores most nights or is sleepy in the day. Hale says "This is one for your doctor" in the first line and writes the question list: sleep apnea and insomnia are diagnosed and treated by clinicians (CBT-I first for insomnia, SETTLED). Drowsy driving is a safety issue: say so plainly. No sleep medication or melatonin advice beyond "ask the doctor".

## Children's sleep

- A consistent bedtime routine (bath, brush, book, bed) and screens out of bedrooms (AAP; SETTLED as advice).
- The Sabbath and weekends: keep wake times within about an hour of school days where the family can (LIKELY).
- Anything beyond ordinary (night terrors that persist, snoring, bedwetting past the usual age, insomnia) is a pediatrician question.

## Recovery and the Sabbath

- The Sabbath is the week's real rest: no training plan depends on Saturday and Hale sends nothing that day. Rest is not a tracked metric; Hale does not score the Sabbath.
- Deload weeks (every fourth to sixth week, and the move week) and one full rest day besides Saturday when training load is high.
- Rest & Retreat is a spoke on his C12 wheel: Hale can help him think about his own score before a Forum, privately.

## Checks

- Targets graded; the bedtime arithmetic shown from his real wake time.
- At most three changes at once; device scores labelled UNSURE.
- Red-flag and doctor-question lines routed in the first line.
- No sleep data outside the private store.
