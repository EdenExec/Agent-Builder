# Children's development (pediatrician first)

General, age-band guidance for Kev on raising healthy children: milestones, sleep, food, screens, sport and habits. Sources at the AAP, CDC and WHO level, graded. The pediatrician owns every question about one child. Read `memory/seed/guideline-starting-map.md` (Children and Sleep) first.

## By age band (summary; open the source before relying on it)

| Age | Sleep | Activity | Screens | Watch for (ask the pediatrician) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Under 2 | 12 to 16 h (4 to 12 months), 11 to 14 h (1 to 2) | Floor play, tummy time, active play | None under 18 months except video chat | Missed CDC milestones; the 9, 18, 24 and 30 month screens are the pediatrician's |
| 3 to 5 | 10 to 13 h | Active through the day | About 1 h of quality content, watched together | Speech, play, toileting questions |

| 13 to 18 | 8 to 10 h | Same as 6 to 12 | Same, with more say for the teen | Mood, anxiety and depression (screened yearly from 12), sleep loss, sport injuries |

## Sport and strength for children (SETTLED, AAP)

- Sample several sports before specialising; delaying single-sport specialisation until mid-adolescence lowers overuse injury and burnout.
- Supervised, technique-first strength training is safe and helpful from about 7 or 8 when a child can follow instructions; no maximal lifts until skeletal maturity unless a qualified coach supervises.
- Rest: at least one to two days a week off organised sport, and time off each year.
- Head injury: any suspected concussion means out of play the same day and a clinician's clearance before return (SETTLED, CDC HEADS UP). Red flags go to `memory/seed/red-flags.md`.

## Screens and the family media plan

Use the AAP Family Media Plan (healthychildren.org) as the template: where devices live overnight, screen-free meals and bedrooms, what counts as good content, and what comes first (sleep, homework, outside, family). Kev and Ally set the rules; Hale drafts options.

## Working with Atlas

When a child should learn something (how sleep grows the brain, why muscles need protein, reading a label, what a vaccine does), Hale writes a short brief: topic, age band, three graded facts with sources, any safety note. It goes to Atlas through Radar or as an ask card on Atlas's Desk. It never carries a child's own sleep, weight, food or symptoms. Atlas builds the lesson; Hale checks the facts if asked. Nothing about food or body is ever paid through the Atlas Bank.

## Steps for a request

1. Read the question for red flags first. Any match: the red-flag line, nothing else.
2. Name the age band (from the private store, age only) and the guideline, graded.
3. Give two or three practical moves for the family.
4. Close with what to ask the pediatrician, and add it to the next visit's list in the private store.

## Checks

- Pediatrician named as the decider on anything about one child.
- Every line graded and sourced at AAP, CDC or WHO level.
- No child's details outside the private store; nothing a child might see comments on their body.
