# Nutrition (simple family meal frameworks)

Food that fuels a working week, feeds growing children and gets the protein Kev's training needs, without a fad, a points system or a forbidden list. Read `memory/seed/guideline-starting-map.md` (Food and drink) first.

## Principles (graded)

- **Pattern over rules.** Mediterranean-style or DASH-style eating improves cardiovascular risk factors (SETTLED, AHA). Mostly whole foods, plants at every meal, protein at every meal.
- **Protein first.** For Kev, if he trains: about 1.6 g per kg of body weight a day, spread over three or four meals of 30 to 50 g (LIKELY, Morton 2018; ISSN 2017). For the children: the age RDA is easy to reach with ordinary meals; no protein targets, shakes or tracking for a child (SETTLED, DRI; AAP).
- **No fad diets.** Keto, carnivore, extended fasts, detoxes and cleanses get a graded answer if Kev asks (usually UNSURE for long-term outcomes) and a note to involve his doctor before any restrictive diet. Time-restricted eating: UNSURE beyond the calorie effect.
- **Drinks.** Water as the default; for children water and plain milk, juice within AAP limits (SETTLED). Alcohol: less is better (SETTLED for direction, Surgeon General 2025); Hale reports, it does not police.

## The family plate (the framework Hale builds from)

| Part | Share | Kev's version | Children's version |
|---|---|---|---|
| Protein | A palm or two | Eggs, Greek yogurt, chicken, beef, fish, beans | Same food, child portions |
| Vegetables and fruit | Half the plate | Two colours at dinner | One they like plus one to try, no pressure |
| Starch | A fist | Potatoes, rice, oats, bread, more on training days | As appetite asks |
| Fat | A thumb | Olive oil, nuts, avocado, butter in cooking | Same |

## Steps

1. **Ask, in one batch:** who cooks and when, the busy nights, foods each person will not eat, any allergies or clinician-advised diets (recorded only in the private store), the grocery rhythm. No calorie counting unless Kev asks for it and his doctor agrees.
2. **Build the week:** a rotation of 8 to 10 dinners that take 30 minutes or less, two big-batch cooks (Sunday after Main Street, Wednesday), three default breakfasts with 30 g or more of protein, a lunch template for the office, a short list of after-school snacks. Mark the Sabbath: Saturday food is prepared ahead or simple, so no one works the kitchen to hit a number.
3. **Protein check for Kev:** show the day's grams by meal against his target in a small table (in the private store). If the gap is large, add food first; a protein powder is a suggestion with a graded note, never a brand or a purchase.
4. **Grocery list:** grouped by store aisle, in the private store or as a plain list Kev can paste. Ordering groceries or meal kits is a `spend` QC item.

## Children and food (pediatrician first)

- The parent decides what, when and where; the child decides whether and how much (Ellyn Satter's division of responsibility; LIKELY, widely used by AAP-aligned dietitians).
- No food as reward or punishment, no comments on a child's body or weight, no "good" and "bad" foods in front of them, and nothing about food or weight tied to the Atlas Bank or any money.
- Picky eating, a growth question, a suspected allergy or any weight concern goes to the pediatrician as a question list.

## Supplements

Report the evidence, graded, from NIH Office of Dietary Supplements fact sheets and systematic reviews (creatine for strength: LIKELY; vitamin D testing in healthy adults: not routinely advised; most "longevity" stacks: UNSURE). Every supplement note ends: "Check with your doctor or pharmacist, especially with any medication." Never a brand, a shop, a dose change or a purchase.

## Checks

- Every target graded and sourced; no fad framing.
- Busy nights and the Sabbath planned for; a child's portion and freedom respected.
- Allergies and diets only in the private store; nothing bought.
