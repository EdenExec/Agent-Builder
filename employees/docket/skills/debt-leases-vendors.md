# The money paper: investor notes, the Boise lease, vendor and software terms

## Investor notes (the $150,000 received in February and March)

Interest is paid at 12% a year, 1% a month flat, and the notes run to about February 2028. Chris Catalano and Joel need the notes in the books and the file.

- **Ask for the paper first.** Is there a signed promissory note for each lender? Principal, rate, how interest accrues (flat monthly on the original principal is not the same as amortising), payment dates, maturity, prepayment, what happens on default, who the parties are (the right entity), whether any note is secured or convertible, whether anyone gets equity or a say.
- **Reconcile to the books:** $1,000 and $250 a month implies principals of $100,000 and $25,000 at 1% flat; $150,000 came in. Name the difference and ask Joel. Tally records it; the gate checks it.
- **Law to research and cite:** interest-rate limits (usury) in the governing state, securities rules for raising money from individuals (exemptions and any required notices), and interest-reporting rules for payments to individuals. All of these depend on the state and the facts, so grade confidence and send it to OUTSIDE COUNSEL and Chris. Do not tell Kev a rule is settled that you only found on one page.
- **If there is no note,** draft one in plain English for Kev to review with counsel: parties, principal, rate and how computed, dates, maturity, payment schedule, default, notices, governing law. Do not send it to a lender.

## The Boise office lease (planned for late November or December)

Rent about $3,000 a month plus utilities and expenses. Before Kev signs anything: term and renewal, rent escalators, who pays what (triple net, common-area charges, utilities, insurance, taxes), security deposit and its return, tenant improvements, subletting, personal guarantee (decide deliberately), early exit, how the entity is named, permitted use, signage and parking. Compare the first-year cost with the placeholder in the forecast and give Tally the exact figures through the gate. Idaho registration, payroll and tax effects of a new location belong on Chris's list; flag them, do not answer them.

## Vendors and software

Auto-renewals and their notice windows, price increases, minimum commitments, seat counts and who may use a seat, data and confidentiality terms, liability caps, termination and refunds, and what happens to data on exit. For every recurring vendor, record the renewal date and the notice deadline in the risk register so a renewal is a decision, not a surprise. LinkedIn bills quarterly and rises; list the dates and terms with Tally.

## Output

Per document: verdict, the numbers table, redlines, open questions. Money terms go through the gate. Anything with a lender, a landlord or a regulator is OUTSIDE COUNSEL by default.
