# Capital-allocation review: the standing question on every Tally packet

Kev, 21 October 2026: Docket "should be looking at Tally's work and always thinking 'is this the smartest way for Eden to be leveraging cash flow as a tool to grow the umbrella.'" The umbrella is Eden Partner Group, Eden Executive Search and the KDTW Group family office.

The numbers gate proves Tally's figures match their sources. This review asks whether the money is being put to its best use. Run it after the gate on every monthly packet, cash forecast and budget vs actual, and on any one-off decision that moves more than one month of fixed costs (a hire, a lease, a loan, a distribution, a new venture). Sources and grades are in `memory/seed/library.md`.

## Order of work (always in this order; inversion comes first)

### 1. Invert: what would kill us?

Munger: "Invert, always invert" (Harvard School, 13 June 1986), and the rustic who wished he knew where he would die "and then I'd never go there". Before asking how to grow, list how Eden dies. Score each kill item yes, no or unknown from the packet.

| Kill item | The test |
|---|---|
| Missed payroll | Can Eden meet the next two payroll dates, the partner wires and the next tax deposit if no client pays for 60 days? |
| One client's default | Does any single receivable exceed one month of fixed outflows? Name it (Baker is the live case). |
| Maturity wall | Is any principal due within 24 months (the investor notes, by February 2028) with no reserve building toward it? |
| Fixed cost before revenue | Does the decision add a fixed monthly cost (the Boise lease) before the revenue that pays for it is signed? |
| Personal guarantee or recourse | Does any loan, lease, card or factoring deal reach Kev personally or let a lender claw back cash? |
| Entity bleed | Does cash cross between EPG, EES or KDTW Group without a document (a distribution, a capital contribution or a loan note)? Co-mingling threatens the liability shield and the books. |
| Tax trap | Are distributions running ahead of the tax reserve? Are distributions pro rata (Kev's standing position; Chris confirms the S-corporation rule)? |
| Paper liability | Could a classification ruling, a void clawback or an unpaid fee dispute create a bill the forecast does not show? |

Buffett, 2009 letter: Berkshire will "never become dependent on the kindness of strangers" and will keep its cash needs "dwarfed by our own liquidity". 2010 letter: "credit is like oxygen", and "leverage all too often produces zeroes, even when it is employed by very smart people". Any yes on this table is the headline of the memo, ahead of every growth idea.

### 2. Margin of safety: the cash floor

Propose a written floor for each entity: the cash Eden never spends below (Berkshire's version is "at least $20 billion on hand", 2010 letter). Until Kev sets it, Docket proposes: the next 90 days of fixed outflows, plus the next tax payment, plus accrued note interest, all taken from Tally's gated forecast. Label it PROPOSED. Every recommendation in the memo must leave the floor intact on the "confirmed" cash line, not the "hopeful" one.

### 3. Opportunity cost: what is the next-best use of this dollar?

Munger: "life is a whole series of opportunity costs" (Berkshire meeting, 1997, as widely reported; LIKELY). Eden already has a price on money: the investor notes cost 12% a year. So a discretionary dollar must beat 12% with margin to spare, or it should be held toward the February 2028 principal. Check the notes for a prepayment right before recommending early repayment; that is a paper question, and it is Docket's.

Rank the uses Thorndike's capital allocators chose from (*The Outsiders*, 2012: reinvest, acquire, pay down debt, pay owners, buy back), translated for Eden:

| Use | When it wins |
|---|---|
| Keep the floor | Always first. |
| Collect faster | Free money: every day cut from DSO releases cash with no cost. Always second. |
| Reinvest in a recruiter or a tool | When the cash payback (below) is under 12 months on the confirmed line. |
| Pay down the 12% notes | When no reinvestment clears 12% with margin, and the notes allow prepayment. |
| Distribute to owners | After the floor and the tax reserve, pro rata, documented. |
| Acquire (a desk, a book of business, a small firm) or a new venture (Boise, a family-office project) | Only inside the circle of competence, sized so that total loss breaks no kill item. |

### 4. Float: who holds whose money?

Buffett, 2009 letter: float is money Eden holds "that will eventually go to others", and it is valuable only if it costs less than other money. Eden is a services firm, so its cash conversion cycle is DSO minus the days it takes to pay its own people and partners. Payroll and partner wires go out on fixed dates, so DSO decides the cycle.

Find every place Eden gives float away and every place it could take float in. Most of these are contract terms, which is why this review is Docket's:

- **Take float in:** engagement retainers paid before work starts; retained search billed in thirds at engagement, shortlist and offer; invoices raised on offer acceptance, not start date; shorter net terms; late-payment interest that the client actually agreed to.
- **Stop giving float away:** commissions and partner splits paid on cash collected, not on billing; no advances off the payroll system; client terms in the Hub matching the signed contract (a Net 90 in the contract against Net 30 in the Hub is a forecast error).
- **Price borrowed float:** factoring at "2% for 30 days" is roughly 24% a year before fees (vendor sources, LIKELY), double the investor notes. An asset-based line is cheaper but carries covenants and reporting. Either is acceptable only to bridge receivables from creditworthy clients, never to fund losses or fixed costs. Read the recourse, reserve and personal-guarantee terms before any number goes in the forecast.

### 5. Incentives: what behaviour is this money paying for?

Munger, 1995: under-recognising incentives is the first standard cause of misjudgment. For each pay line in the packet, ask what it rewards.

- Commission on billing rewards placements that fall off. Commission on collection, with a clawback tied to a fall-off or guarantee refund, rewards fees that stick.
- Washington ESHB 1155 (Chapter 149, Laws of 2026, effective 30 June 2027) treats any clause that makes a worker "return, repay, or forfeit" pay because they work elsewhere as a void noncompete, including covenants signed before that date. A clawback must be tied to the placement failing, never to the recruiter leaving or competing. SETTLED (final bill report, read 21 October 2026).
- Early-pay discounts and late fees change a client's incentive to pay. Price them against the 12% cost of money.

### 6. Compounding, patience and the tax trap

- Munger, 1994: the wise "bet big when they have the odds" and otherwise sit still. Few big decisions, well made, beat many small ones. Say "hold" when nothing clears the bar.
- Munger, 1994: "trying to minimize taxes too much is one of the great standard causes of really dumb mistakes". Tally's tax-strategy mandate is legitimate; Docket's job is to check that no tax idea spends cash, adds leverage or adds paper risk out of proportion to the saving. A tax saving that breaks a kill item is a loss.
- Thorndike's allocators judged themselves by cash flow per owner, not reported earnings or headcount. Ask whether a decision grows cash per owner, not just revenue.

### 7. Circle of competence

Munger, 1994: "you've got to play within your own circle of competence." Recruiting and executive search are the circle. A family-office or real-estate venture (the Vancouver short-term rental under KDTW Group) sits outside it. It takes its own entity, its own cash and its own paper, and any Eden money that funds it moves by a written, arm's-length loan or a documented distribution, never by a transfer.

## The recruiter-hire payback test

Cash payback, not accrual payback. Month by month from the start date, add the fully loaded cost (pay, payroll tax, tools, seat). Then add collected gross margin: placements times average fee, after the partner split, landing at the start date plus that client's actual DSO, not the contract terms. Payback is the first month where cumulative cash in exceeds cumulative cash out. Ramp comes from Eden's own history (Dean's onboarding ramp). The generic sales curve of 25%, 50% and 75% productivity in months one to three is a placeholder only (Xactly via a staffing blog, UNSURE for recruiters). Every input is a number, so it goes through the gate.

## Output: the capital-allocation memo (one page, to Radar)

1. **Verdict line:** SMART (best use, keep going), SHARPEN (a better use exists, named) or DANGER (a kill item is live).
2. **Kill list:** any yes or unknown from step 1, with the dollar exposure (gated).
3. **Three levers**, ranked by cash effect: what to change, the paper that changes it (clause or new document), the cash effect and when it lands. Every figure carries its gate id.
4. **The one question for Kev.**
5. **Where Docket and Tally disagree:** both positions, the evidence, who decides (Kev, Chris or outside counsel).

Score it with `rubrics/capital-allocation.md` before it leaves. The memo is analysis only. It moves no money, files nothing and gives no investment, tax or legal advice. Investment and tax calls go to Kev and Chris, and legal exposure goes to outside counsel per `risk-register.md`.
