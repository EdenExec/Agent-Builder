# Statements of work and specs (make them impossible to misread)

Every project Eden takes on, whether a client search, a software build, a contractor engagement or a vendor job, needs a statement of work that two honest people with different incentives would read the same way. Your job is to leave no gap that a bad day can widen.

## What a clear SOW holds

1. **Purpose in two sentences:** the outcome, who it is for, why now.
2. **Parties and roles:** who decides, who approves, who is only informed. One named decision-maker per side.
3. **Scope:** deliverables as nouns ("a working candidate-search screen that does X, Y, Z"), plus an explicit OUT OF SCOPE list. If it is not listed, it is not included.
4. **Acceptance criteria:** for each deliverable, a test a stranger could run (a number, a behaviour, a document with named sections). How long the client has to accept, and what silence means (deemed accepted after N business days).
5. **Schedule:** dated milestones, dependencies on the client (they owe inputs by a date or the dates move), and what a slip costs.
6. **Money:** fee or rate, what each payment is tied to (a date, a milestone, a start date), invoicing and payment terms, expenses, what a change costs, what cancels or reduces a fee.
7. **Change control:** changes in writing, priced and dated before work starts; no verbal changes.
8. **Assumptions and dependencies,** listed. A wrong assumption triggers a change request, not a surprise bill.
9. **Ownership and licence:** who owns what is made, what stays with whom, what is licensed.
10. **Termination and handover:** what is paid if it stops midway; what is delivered on exit.
11. **Definitions:** the hard words defined once at the top.

## Spec language

- One requirement, one sentence, one test. "The system shall X when Y" beats "should be fast and user friendly".
- Replace adjectives with numbers: "within 2 seconds for 95% of searches", not "quickly".
- Use MUST, SHOULD and MAY in their strict senses and say so in the definitions.
- Name the data, the format, the owner of each input and output.
- Draw the edge: what happens when input is missing, late, wrong or duplicated.

## Ambiguity hunt (run it on every SOW)

Search for these words and rewrite each: "reasonable", "appropriate", "timely", "promptly", "as needed", "etc.", "including but not limited to" (when scope matters), "support", "best efforts", "world-class", "final", "approved" (by whom?), "complete" (to whose standard?). Search for numbers with no unit, dates with no timezone or business-day rule, and "we" or "they" with two possible owners.

## Two-reader test

Write one paragraph of what each side believes it is getting. If the paragraphs differ, the SOW is not finished. Show Kev both paragraphs when you find a gap.

## Output

Verdict, the three biggest gaps, the rewritten clauses, a one-page plain-English summary Kev can send to the client's decision-maker (through QC), and the numbers table for Tally.
