# The people paper: contractors, commissions, W-2 versus 1099, restrictive terms

Eden pays W-2 employees through Gusto and some recruiters as 1099 contractors who are moving onto commission. Kev and Nick will set the commission plan; until they do, nothing about it is assumed (the Hub split percentages describe billing credit, not pay).

## When Kev asks for a commission plan or contractor agreement

1. **Get the plan in numbers first.** Rate or tier, what it applies to (billed, collected, started), when it is earned, when it is paid, what happens on a candidate fall-off or guarantee refund (clawback and offset), what happens on termination. A plan that says "commission on placements" is not a plan.
2. **Define the words money turns on:** "placement", "collected", "start date", "guarantee period", "net fee" (after what), "split".
3. **Write it as its own document or an addendum** so the commission can change without reopening the contractor agreement; changes only in writing, prospective unless both sign.
4. **Show Kev the cost** of the plan at three revenue levels (low, plan, high) and send the table through the numbers gate to Tally, who owns the forecast.

## Classification risk (1099 versus W-2)

Control and independence decide it, not the label. Facts that lean toward employee: set hours, Eden's tools and systems, training, an ongoing role integral to the business, exclusivity, no other clients, no risk of loss. Several tests exist (the IRS common-law factors, state unemployment and wage tests, and in some states an "ABC" style test); the right one depends on the state and the purpose. Research the current test for each state a contractor works in, write down which facts Eden controls, and flag **OUTSIDE COUNSEL** when a contractor looks like an employee (full-time, Eden systems, one client). Misclassification can bring back payroll taxes, penalties, benefits and wage claims.

## Terms to include for contractors

Services and deliverables, term, fees and the commission addendum, invoicing and payment timing, independent status, expenses, confidentiality (candidate and client data), ownership of work product, return of materials, non-solicit of clients and candidates, termination and handover, governing law. Keep restrictive terms narrow and supportable; some states limit or void non-competes and require notices or minimum pay thresholds, so check the current rule for the state before relying on one (Washington, Oregon and Idaho each have their own).

## W-2 paper

Offer letters and commission or bonus plans for employees: at-will language, plan documents, how advances are recovered (written authorisation is required in some states before deducting from pay, so research the state before any recoupment from wages). Flag any advance made outside payroll.

## Output

A one-page summary for Kev (the deal in plain English, what it costs, the open choices), the draft or redlines, the classification notes, and the numbers table for Tally. Never contact a contractor, never send a draft; queue it through QC.
