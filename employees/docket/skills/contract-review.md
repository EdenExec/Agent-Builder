# Contract review (any agreement, in or out)

Read as the other side's lawyer would, then as a judge who has never met either party. Output the verdict first, then the redlines.

## Order of work

1. **Identify** the document, the parties (exact legal names and entities, checked against the books: Eden Partner Group LLC vs Eden Executive Search), the date, the governing law and venue, and who is asking you for what.
2. **One-line verdict:** SIGN, FIX FIRST, DO NOT SIGN, or NEEDS COUNSEL, with the reason.
3. **Top three issues**, ranked by dollars at risk times likelihood. Each: the clause, what is wrong, what it costs if it goes wrong, the fix.
4. **Redlines**: exact current wording, exact proposed wording, one-line reason. Group as MUST (do not sign without), SHOULD (worth the ask), NICE (trade away first).
5. **Questions for Kev**, ranked, each with your assumption so work does not stall.
6. **Numbers table**: every fee, rate, date, term and total in the document with the clause number, recomputed by you, then sent to Tally through the numbers gate before the review leaves you.

## The checklist (tick each, say "n/a" or "missing")

- **Parties and authority:** correct names, who signs, authority to bind, affiliates covered or excluded.
- **Definitions:** every capitalised term defined once, used the same way. Look hard at the words money turns on: "candidate", "hire", "start date", "placement", "compensation", "acceptance", "business day", "affiliate".
- **Money:** amount, formula, currency, when due, what triggers it, late fees and interest, taxes, expenses, set-off, refunds, discounts and what cancels them. Check the arithmetic.
- **Triggers and conditions:** is each obligation a promise or a condition? Who decides whether it is met, by when, and on what evidence?
- **Term and exit:** start, end, renewal (auto-renewal notice windows), termination for cause and convenience, cure periods, what survives, wind-down and handover.
- **Risk allocation:** warranties, indemnities (who, for what, capped?), limitation of liability, exclusions for consequential damages, insurance, force majeure (specific events, notice, a way out).
- **Remedies:** liquidated damages must be a reasonable forecast, not a penalty; fee-shifting; injunctive relief; jury waiver; arbitration versus court; limitation periods.
- **IP and data:** who owns work product, licences back, confidentiality scope and duration, return or destruction, personal data (candidate data is personal data).
- **Restrictive terms:** non-solicit, non-circumvention, exclusivity, non-compete. These are state-law sensitive; research the governing state's current rule before relying on them.
- **Boilerplate that bites:** entire agreement, amendment in writing, assignment, notices (who, how, deemed received), counterparts, severability, order of precedence between the contract, the SOW and any attachments.

## Interpretation habits

Specific beats general. Defined terms beat ordinary meaning. Ambiguity is read against the drafter. A handwritten or later term can override a printed one unless the contract says otherwise. Words like "reasonable", "promptly", "material" and "as needed" are fights waiting to happen: give them a number, a date or a test. Say so, and fix it.

## Output rules

Plain English, short sentences, no Latin where English works. Cite law with the source, date and a confidence grade. If you did not verify it, write "unverified". Mark OUTSIDE COUNSEL when exposure is large (see `risk-register.md`). Never send, sign or accept.
