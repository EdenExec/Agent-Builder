# Recruiting paper: search, fee and placement agreements

Eden earns by placing people, so the agreement decides when the money is real. Read every client agreement for these points, and compare it with what the Hub Rev Report and QuickBooks assume.

## The fee

- **Basis and rate:** percentage of what? First-year base salary, total cash, or target? Are sign-on bonuses, relocation, equity and commission counted? A rate with no base is a dispute. The Hub carries a rate and a salary per placement; the contract must match.
- **Trigger:** is the fee earned on offer accepted, on signed offer, or on start date? The Hub treats start date as the billing event; confirm the contract does too. What if the candidate starts a different role, in a different entity, or as a contractor first?
- **Due date and terms:** Net 15, 30, 45, 90 or "due on start". Check the contract against the "Net Terms" column in the Hub; they disagree often. Late fee and interest, and whether a dispute pauses payment.
- **Discounts:** stated, with the condition that earns them and the one that cancels them (for example a relocation discount that disappears on early termination).
- **Retainers:** credited against the fee or not? Refundable or not? Due when?
- **Splits:** a fee shared with another recruiter or partner is owed by Eden to them under a separate agreement; the client contract must not promise anything about it.

## The guarantee

- Length of the guarantee (days after start), what breaks it (resignation, termination, with or without cause), what Eden owes (replacement search, refund, credit) and the ordering of those remedies.
- A guarantee ties up revenue: ask Tally whether fees inside a guarantee window should be treated as earned. Record the answer for Chris.
- Fall-offs: a candidate who signs and never starts earns no fee; define that in the agreement ("fee is due only on the candidate's actual start date"). A candidate who starts and leaves inside the guarantee triggers replacement or refund. Track both (the finance dashboard counts them).

## Protection

- **Candidate ownership:** how long a presented candidate is Eden's (a tail of 6 to 12 months), what counts as presentation, and a fee if the client hires them later through anyone else.
- **Non-circumvention and non-solicit:** both directions, narrowly worded; check the state's rule before relying on them.
- **Exclusivity and exclusivity windows:** who is bound, for how long, what happens when the client fills the job elsewhere.
- **Confidentiality and candidate data:** how Eden handles candidate information; the client may not forward profiles.

## Collections

Written notice and cure period, interest, costs and attorney fees to the winner, venue near Eden, and a personal guarantee only if offered knowingly. Keep an eye on clients who pay slowly: a 65-day-late invoice is a legal question as well as a cash one, and the contract decides what Eden can do about it.

## Output

For each agreement: the fee formula in one line, the trigger, the terms, the guarantee, the tail, then the redlines. Numbers (rate, base, due date, discount) go through the gate.
