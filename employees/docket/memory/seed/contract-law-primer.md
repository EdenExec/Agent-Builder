# Contract law primer (seeded 17 October 2026)

Working notes for reading and writing Eden's contracts. General principles from US common law and the UCC; every point of law Docket relies on is re-verified for the governing state and dated before it is cited. This is a starting map, not a source.

## Formation
- Offer, acceptance and consideration (something of value exchanged), plus mutual assent on the essential terms. A court will not enforce terms too indefinite to apply: price, subject matter and quantity or duration need to be fixable.
- Writing: some contracts (for example ones that cannot be performed within a year, and sales of goods above a threshold under the UCC) must be in a signed writing to be enforced. Put everything in writing anyway.
- Authority: the person who signs must be able to bind the entity. Name the entity exactly and say who signed in which capacity.

## Interpretation
- Courts start with the words, read the whole document, and give defined terms their definitions. Specific terms beat general ones. Ambiguity is typically read against the drafter (contra proferentem), which is one reason Eden's own paper must be the clearest.
- Parol evidence: a clear integration ("entire agreement") clause generally keeps earlier talk and emails out; so the contract must hold every term that matters, and changes must follow the amendment clause.
- Course of dealing and performance can fill gaps and sometimes override sloppy terms; do not rely on them.

## Performance and breach
- A promise is a covenant (its breach gives damages); a condition must happen first or the other duty never arises. Write which is which. "Time is of the essence" makes dates strict.
- Material breach can excuse the other side; minor breach usually gives damages only. Define material breach and a cure period.
- Remedies: expectation damages, restitution, specific performance (rare), and agreed remedies. Liquidated damages are enforced when they are a reasonable pre-estimate of loss and the loss is hard to measure; a penalty is not. Duty to mitigate usually applies.
- Limitation of liability and exclusion of consequential damages are generally enforced between businesses but not for fraud or wilful misconduct and subject to local limits.

## Words with legal jobs
- "Shall" is a duty, "may" is permission, "will" is ambiguous; use them strictly and say so.
- "Including" is open unless "only" or "without limitation" decides it; "best efforts", "reasonable efforts" and "commercially reasonable efforts" are read differently by different courts; give a test or a number.
- "Terminate", "cancel", "expire", "rescind" are not the same.

## Contracts Eden meets most
- **Services and consulting:** common law governs; scope, acceptance and ownership are the fights.
- **Software and SaaS:** licence terms, renewals, data, liability caps.
- **Employment and contractor:** governed by state and federal statutes that override the contract (wages, classification, restrictive covenants). The label in the contract does not decide the status.
- **Loans:** state interest-rate limits, securities rules for raising money, tax reporting. Verify, then send to counsel.
- **Leases:** real property rules, often strict on notice and remedy.

## Drafting for enforcement
Keep a clean record (versions, who signed, when). Use notices clauses with real addresses. Avoid unsigned changes. Signature blocks name the entity and the signer's role.
