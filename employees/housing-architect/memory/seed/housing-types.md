# Housing types: what matters

## Executive home (single-family, larger, higher finish)
- Arrival: a deliberate sequence from street to door. Garage doors should not be the first thing seen.
- Zones: public (entry, great room, kitchen, dining), private (primary suite, bedrooms), work (home office, ideally separated for acoustics), service (mudroom, pantry, laundry, garage), guest.
- Primary suite as retreat: bedroom, bath, closet, optional sitting area or exterior access, away from the entertaining zone.
- Kitchen: working kitchen plus optional scullery or pantry so the show kitchen stays clean.
- Storage: executives under-provide. Check for coats, seasonal, sports, luggage, bulk goods, files.
- Outdoor rooms: covered, heated or shaded as climate demands, connected to kitchen.
- Resale discipline: unusual features narrow the buyer pool. Flag them, do not forbid them.

## Ranch
- One level or mostly one level. Long low massing, deep eaves, covered porches.
- Strong relationship to land, view and approach. Siting often matters more than the plan.
- Programme variants: linear, L, U or courtyard, split-wing with a great-room spine.
- Outbuildings: shop, barn, guest cottage, equipment, vehicle and trailer turning radii.
- Rural realities: well and septic, fire access, defensible space, snow load, frost depth, propane or heat pump.
- Aging in place: step-free, wide doors, ground-level primary suite.

## Common space targets (rough, UNVERIFIED, adjust to the client)
- Primary suite 450 to 800 sq ft including bath and closet.
- Secondary bedroom 130 to 180 sq ft.
- Great room 400 to 700 sq ft.
- Kitchen 200 to 350 sq ft plus pantry 40 to 120 sq ft.
- Home office 120 to 200 sq ft.
- Mudroom 60 to 120 sq ft.
- Garage bay about 12 x 24 ft each, 3-bay about 36 x 26 ft.
