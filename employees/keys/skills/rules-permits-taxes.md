# Rules, permits, licences and lodging taxes

The question this answers: what must be true, legally, before the first guest arrives, and what does it cost and take. Start from `memory/seed/rules-starting-map.md`, then verify every line against the official source and grade it.

## Order of work

1. **Jurisdiction.** Confirm whether the parcel is inside City of Vancouver limits or unincorporated Clark County (Clark County property information and GIS; the parcel's jurisdiction line). Record the parcel number and zoning. Everything after this depends on the answer.
2. **City or county rules.** For the City of Vancouver: VMC 20.835 (short-term rentals, Ordinance M-4433, December 2023), the short-term rental permit, the city business licence endorsement, the neighbour notice, inspection rights, occupancy and parking standards, renewal, what voids the permit (a change of ownership does). If the parcel is in unincorporated Clark County, find the county code section instead. Read the adopted code, not a draft or a blog.
3. **State rules.** RCW 64.37 (short-term rentals): taxes (64.37.020), safety (64.37.030: smoke and carbon monoxide detectors, fire extinguisher, exits, emergency contacts, posted guest contact information), platform duties (64.37.040), liability insurance of at least $1,000,000 or a platform that provides equal cover (64.37.050). Washington business licence and a UBI through the Business Licensing Service if required.
4. **Taxes.** Washington retail sales tax on stays under 30 nights (state plus local rate for the location code), any special hotel and motel (lodging) tax for the city or county, and the state's transient rental income reporting. Check what Airbnb and VRBO collect and remit as marketplace facilitators in Washington and what remains Kev's to file (direct bookings, any tax a platform does not collect). Stays of 30 nights or more are treated differently; confirm how. Business and occupation (B&O) tax, and the federal side (Schedule E versus C, the 14-day and average-stay rules, depreciation) go to Tally and Chris, not answered by you.
5. **Ownership facts that matter.** Mortgage or deed of trust occupancy clauses, title or entity (personal versus an LLC), and whether moving out changes the homestead or tax status. Flag these for Docket and Tally; do not opine.

## Grading and citing

For each rule: the rule in one line, the source (code section, statute or agency page with link), the date read, the grade (SETTLED, LIKELY, CONTESTED, UNSURE), what it requires, the cost, the lead time, and who acts. A blog or vendor summary is LIKELY at best; an adopted code page or statute read this session is SETTLED.

## Flags for Docket (always)

Permit and licence applications before they are filed, anything that needs a signature or an attestation, the insurance policy wording, HOA covenants, the mortgage clause, property-manager and cleaner agreements, platform terms that shift liability, and anything graded CONTESTED or UNSURE that blocks go-live. Write each as a review request with the document, the question and your read.

## Output

A compliance checklist (rule, source, grade, action, cost, lead time, owner, status), a go-live critical path with dates, and the open legal questions for Docket. You never file, register, apply or pay; each is a QC item (`write_external` for a portal entry, `spend` for a fee).
