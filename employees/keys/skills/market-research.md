# Market research: comps, nightly rate, occupancy, seasonality

The question this answers: what will 600 SE 101st earn as a short-term rental, in which months, against which competitors, and what kind of guest books it.

## Order of work

1. **Know the house first.** Bedrooms, bathrooms, beds, square feet, parking, yard, any hot tub, office, laundry, accessibility, pet suitability. Pull what you can from the county assessor record and ask Kev for the rest in one batch. A comp set is only as good as the subject.
2. **Build the comp set.** 10 to 20 active whole-home listings within about 3 miles (east Vancouver, Cascade Park, Fircrest, Mill Plain, Orchards) with the same bedroom count plus or minus one. Record per comp: platform, link, bedrooms, sleeps, nightly rate on sample dates, cleaning fee, minimum stay, review count and score, amenities that stand out. Read listing pages only; never message a host.
3. **Rates and occupancy.** Sample the public calendars and prices for four dates per season (a weekday and a weekend in mid-month, a holiday, a big Portland event weekend). Occupancy from calendars is an estimate; label it so. Paid data (AirDNA, PriceLabs, Rabbu) is a QC spend item with the price and what it adds; until Kev says yes, use free previews and mark them LIKELY.
4. **Seasonality.** Monthly table: expected ADR, occupancy and nights booked, with the reason (summer peak, holidays, school breaks, Portland events, Columbia River Gorge season, winter low). State the source of each.
5. **Demand drivers.** Who books east Vancouver: visiting family, travel nurses and contractors on 30-plus-night stays (PeaceHealth Southwest and Legacy Salmon Creek), business travellers near PDX airport, Gorge and Mount Hood weekenders, Portland event visitors. Note which ones a mid-term (30-night) strategy would capture, because a 30-plus-night stay changes the tax and permit picture (see `rules-permits-taxes.md`).

## Output

- A one-page market read: result first (projected gross per year, low, base and high), the comp table, the monthly seasonality table, the three demand drivers that matter.
- Every number has a source and a date. Comps are listed by link, never with a host's name or photos.
- Feed the base case into the pro forma in `str-tracker.md` and open a numbers gate with Tally for the go or no-go figures.

## Traps

- Asking prices are not booked prices. A full-looking calendar may be blocked dates.
- One comp with a hot tub or a view skews the average; show the median.
- Short-term versus mid-term is a strategy choice for Kev; show both, with the rules and taxes each triggers.
